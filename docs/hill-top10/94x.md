# Koenigstuhl 94x hill, top 10: rebuilt from blocks

The X hill: ICWS'94, 55,440 cells, 500,000 cycles, 10,000 processes, length 200, P-space allowed. Every number here was measured with `--rules 94x-hill` (the hill itself, with P-space); `--rules 94x` is the same rule set without P-space and was not used. The line-by-line reviews in [../hill-top30/94x.md](../hill-top30/94x.md) are the starting point; this note adds what running the originals and the rebuilds showed.

How it was measured:

- **Hill score**: points a round against the hill's top 30 (`hill <warrior> --hill koenigstuhl:94x --top 30 --rules 94x-hill --rounds 50`, seed 1), for the original and for the rebuild. 50 rounds a pairing is 1,500 rounds in all; differences under about 0.05 are noise.
- **Head to head**: `battle rebuild original --rules 94x-hill -r 100`, the rebuild's wins-losses-ties.
- **Arms**: a switcher's components were measured alone by giving a scratch copy of the original another `org` (never added to the repo), and each rebuilt arm as its own tree (20 or 30 rounds a pairing).
- **ModelWar**: each recipe built with `--rules modelwar --length 5040` (distances rescaled from 55,440, steps moved to the nearest value with the same gcd that the blocks accept) and played against the 17-warrior ModelWar field, 100 rounds each way, seed 1. References on the same field: Decoy Adapter v6.1 2.16, Trio v10 1.98, runner-stone-imp 1.67, runner-paper-stone 1.69, the Void Runner port 1.68, the Azathoth port 1.42.

The recipes are `top10-94x-<name>` in `tools/algebra/recipes-top10/94x.js`; `node scripts/algebra.js recipe top10-94x-ballista --rules 94x-hill --length 200` builds one. Four blocks were added, in `tools/algebra/blocks/top10-94x.js`: `airbag-mov-stone`, `flag-cmp-scanner`, `stone-dual-pump` and `arrow-scanner`.

## 1. Fatamorgana X (Zul Nadzri)

**What it is.** A 200-cell P-space switcher over four warriors: Moore's airbag stone (identical to Fire and Ice II's), a two-pair compare scanner (Recon 2), a nine-process cc paper and The Truth Is Out There II. Two engines run in front of them: "SureWin" (a self-recognition handshake) and "Predator" (score boxes).

**How it works.**

1. *SureWin, round 1.* P-cell 0 reads -1. It stores a marker (300) in P-cell 300, then scans backward from 210 cells below its `count` line, one cell a cycle (`jmz.f seek, {count`), for the first non-empty cell. The `sne.i #pmula, {count` compares the cell one further back with its own `count` instruction. A match means the cell above is the end of another Fatamorgana: it parks in `jmp $0`. Anything else: it falls off the end of its image and dies. Against every other opponent it loses round 1 on purpose.
2. *SureWin, round 2.* The result is a loss and the marker is still set: it resets the marker (`stp #1, #pbox`) and executes a DAT. Round 2 is thrown too. From round 3 the marker no longer matches and control passes to Predator. Measured against Tsunami in the earlier review: round 1 lost at cycle 494, round 2 at cycle 8. Between two copies the one that parks wins round 1, and the loser throws round 2; what this buys on a hill that does not score self-play is **uncertain**.
3. *Predator.* P-cell 15 holds a brainwash sentinel (651); a wrong value resets every box (`bwash`: zeroes cells 11 onward, writes the sentinel, sets the active warrior to 1). Each warrior has a score box. After a win the box of the warrior just played gets +1; after a loss `sub.a #2` then `add.a #1` makes -1; a tie leaves the box alone and replays the same warrior, but a draw counter (P-cell 45) turns the fourth tie in a row into a loss. Then a chain of `slt.a` picks the warrior with the highest box and stores it as active.
4. *Arms.* The stone is booted 43,990 cells away; Recon 2 27,720 away (.8c, step 4876, pair gap 6, SPL wipe, a 25,000-loop timer to a DAT clear); the paper copies a nine-line body 9,108 away and runs three silk stages (9,117, 4,730, 777) ending in a backward `dat {34117, <34117` stream; Truth II 19,255 away. After booting, the scanner arms copy or blank a handful of cells of the abandoned image (`mov gula, ...`, `mov X, X+6`); purpose **uncertain**.

**Why it is strong.** The arms are the hill's best (the stone alone scores more than any whole switcher, see #2), and the score boxes keep a winner for as long as it keeps winning.

**Rebuild.** `switch-score` (WIN 1, TIE 0, LOSS -1, STREAK 4, sentinel 651, the stone first) over `boot(airbag-mov-stone)`, a booted `pair-wipe-scanner` (step 4876, gap 6) for Recon 2, a booted `silk-cc` (the paper's three steps), and a booted `follow-scanner` for Truth II. 199 cells. SureWin is left out: the gate refuses a warrior that kills itself, and here that is the point of it.

**Fidelity.** 1.50 against the original's 1.37. The difference is SureWin: in a 50-round pairing two thrown rounds cost 0.12 points a round. Head to head the rebuild still loses, 38-48-14, so the arms the original carries (its Recon 2 and its paper in particular) are better than our stand-ins.

**ModelWar.** 1.59. Above Decoy Adapter v6.1 against Drift v2 (2.06 / 1.76) and MWClient (2.25 / 2.17); below 0.8 against the Lisan and orn family, Mercenary and InnovationStation, which beat the stone, the paper and both scanners alike.

**Lessons.** `switch-score` already expresses Predator almost exactly (score per arm, draw streak as a loss, sentinel reset). A handshake that throws rounds is a cost on any hill that does not reward beating yourself; on a 50-round pairing it costs a tenth of a point.

## 2. Fire and Ice II (David Moore)

**What it is.** A P-space switcher with two arms: The Truth Is Out There II (a scanner that answers imps) and Moore's airbag stone, with 71 and 56 blank cells between brain and arms.

**How it works.**

1. *The P^3 brain* (five lines and a table). `ldp.a #0, pTab2` puts last round's result in pTab2's A-field; `ldp.a #2001, pTable` puts the stored state in pTable's A-field; `mod.ba *pTab2, pTable` reduces it by the B-field of the row the result points to: 6 after a loss, 7 after a win, 5 after a tie, and 1 on the first round (the -1 result points at the `spl #1, 1` above the moduli), so the first round always gives row 0. `stp.b *pTable, #2001` stores that row's B-field as the next state. `jmp }0` then jumps twice indirect: pTable's A-field (the row number r) is also the operand, so the jump goes through row r's A-field to the arm. Row 0 is pTable itself: the `}` bumps it to 1 and the second pass lands on row 1, the scanner. Each stored number is chosen by the Chinese remainder theorem so that its residues mod 5, 6 and 7 are the next rows after a tie, a loss and a win (155 = 31·5 + 0 = 25·6 + 5 = 22·7 + 1). Probed in the VM for every state and result: rows 0 to 2 play the scanner, 3 to 5 the stone; transitions (win / loss / tie) 0: 1/5/0, 1: 2/0/1, 2: 2/1/2, 3: 3/4/4, 4: 3/5/4, 5: 3/0/0. The scanner needs three losses to leave from its top row, the stone three from its top row; a stone entered after one scanner loss (row 5) is on probation and leaves after one more loss or tie.
2. *The airbag stone.* A two-part boot puts the four-line loop 43,990 cells away with five processes, the remote bomb `mov 53421, 1` eleven cells after the loop's top, and a copy of the d-clear at the loop's pointer line + 53,421 (2,019 cells back). Each pass: `add.ab #2·53421, dPtr` moves the pointer; `mov >2, @dPtr` copies the remote bomb to X through the JMN's B-field and bumps that field; `mov <1, *(2·53421)` puts it back and copies the bomb again to X + 53,421, through the A-field of the bomb it has just written; `jmn.b dTop, dTop+dbPos` checks the remote bomb's B-field. The five processes run the loop in order, so the one shared pointer reads right. 53,421 shares 3 with the core: the lattice is 18,480 cells, two a pass, finished in 9,240 passes (36,960 cycles, measured). Then the pointer is 0 again and the first MOV writes the bomb onto its own pointer line. The next process to run that line executes `mov 53421, 1`, which copies the d-clear's SPL (2,019 back) over the JMN: from then on every process that reaches the JMN line splits there, and its JMP feeds the d-clear (`spl #0` / `mov bomb, >gate` / `djn.f -1, >gate`, bomb `dat <27721`). Measured: at cycle 37,000 the d-clear holds 10 processes, at 60,000 1,800. If an enemy zeroes the remote bomb's B-field, the JMN falls through at once (the airbag). The MOV bomb does not kill: a process that runs it copies the cell 2,019 back (usually another bomb) over the next cell and runs into that, so it keeps copying bombs forward instead of running its own code until the d-clear arrives. That last effect is reasoned from the code, not traced.
3. *Truth II.* One process, booted 19,241 away; the boot then erases its pointers with `mod.f`. A .5c F-scan (step 13,379: `add.a #13378` and a `}` on the `jmz.f`). On a find it walks back a cell a cycle to the start of the object, lays SPLs forward until the first empty cell, drops `stp.ab >0, #0` (a brainwash bomb: processes stunned on the SPLs run it and overwrite their own P-space), then adds the found cell's B-field to the pointer: on an imp that is the ring step, so it lands on the next arm of the ring. After a lap it finds itself and drops into `spl #0` + d-clear.

**Why it is strong.** The stone alone is the strongest component on the hill: Fire and Ice II run as the stone only (a scratch copy with `org dBoot`) scores **1.66**, more than the whole warrior (1.57); the scanner alone scores 0.86. The brain costs a little against this field because it opens with the scanner and must lose to find the stone; it pays against opponents that beat the stone.

**Rebuild.** `switch-table` (PACKED 1, the P^3 encoding) over `boot(airbag-mov-stone)` and `boot(follow-scanner STP 1)`. `switch-table` has four states, Moore's table six, so the rebuild keeps two strikes per arm: scanner strong / weak (first), stone strong / on probation. Rows: after a loss S0 to T0, S1 to S0, T1 to T0, T0 to S0; after a win back to the strong row of the arm played; after a tie stay, except T0 to S0.

**New block: `airbag-mov-stone`.** The catalog's `remote-bomb-stone` writes a DAT and a blank; booted the same way it scored 0.78 alone where the original stone scored 1.66. The new block is Moore's loop: the MOV bomb written twice a pass through its own A-field, the shared JMN pointer, and the time-up through its own pointer line into a SPL-fed d-clear planted D cells away. Alone on the hill (30 rounds a pairing): in place 1.33; booted whole by the `boot` combinator 1.40; with `HOP` (copy only the six loop cells and the bomb, plant the clear, leave everything else behind) 1.56; `HOP` inside a `boot` (as the recipes use it) 1.53; the original stone with the rest of its image stripped 1.63. Booted whole against `HOP`, 1.40 against 1.56, is the footprint: a 29-cell block lands in the far core as one solid object, and a wipe-until-empty scanner that finds its dead boot cells wipes straight on into the loop. Moore's loop sits alone: five cells, then blanks.

**Fidelity.** 1.40 / 1.57, 41-46-13. Arms alone: stone 1.53 / 1.66, scanner 0.59 / 0.86. The follower is the weak part.

**ModelWar.** 1.63. Above Decoy Adapter v6.1 against Drift v2 (2.02 against 1.76), Drift (2.25 / 2.02) and clu_3 (1.90 / 1.73); far below it against the Lisan and orn family (0.70 to 0.93 against 1.88 to 2.10), Mercenary (0.55) and InnovationStation (0.41).

**Lessons.** (1) Moore's stone arm on its own outscores every switcher in the top 5: a stone that converts processes with a MOV bomb, watches one remote cell, and ends in a SPL-fed d-clear exactly when its lattice is done. (2) Booted code should leave only live cells in the far core. (3) The P^3 table needs six states; `switch-table`'s four lose the probation row of the stone and the third strike of the scanner.

## 3. Fire and Brimstone (David Moore / Steve Gunnell)

**What it is.** Fire and Ice II with Kenshin in place of Truth II (state cell 123), so the first round and every no-P-space round play Kenshin.

**How it works.** Brain and stone as #2. Kenshin is booted copy-ahead: `spl LEAP, head` starts the copy 32,003 away at once and ten `mov.i >sBoot, }sBoot` copy behind the running child, then `sub.f` erases the pointer. One process runs three instructions that do two jobs: `mov *tail, }bptr` lays one carpet cell at a running pointer, `add.a #15331, @hptr` / `jmz.f trash, *head` tests one cell (.33c scan, step 15,331, coprime). A find copies the scan pointer into the carpet pointer (`mov.a head, bptr`, skipped by `slt` when the find is among its own 14 cells), so the carpet always runs and is re-aimed at each find. Each time the scan pointer comes round (166,320 cycles) `jmp @hptr, }tail` moves the bomb along a ladder: `spl #0, }0`, then `jmp #0, }0`, then `dat 0, 0`: three passes fit in 500,000 cycles.

**Why it is strong.** The carpet never stops, so an opponent's pieces get stunned between finds; the ladder turns the stun field into a kill field a lap later.

**Rebuild.** As #2 with `boot(EARLY 1)` of `ladder-scanner` (step 15,331, no STP rung, as Brimstone uses JMP).

**Fidelity.** 1.35 / 1.49, 38-45-17.

**ModelWar.** 1.72, the best of the Moore switchers there; above Decoy Adapter v6.1 against Drift v2, Drift, clu_3 and InnovationStation (1.68 / 1.54).

**Lessons.** The arm swap is the whole difference between #2 and #3; the brain and the stone are byte-identical. A switcher is a frame for arms.

## 4. Ballista (Christian Schmidt)

**What it is.** Fire and Ice II with Arrow X (#15 on the hill alone) as the scanner arm.

**How it works.** Brain and stone as #2. Arrow X is booted 18,008 away by a copy-ahead boot whose pointer is then scrambled with `mod.ab`. Arrow X is 11 lines and one process:

1. It enters at `sScan`: a three-instruction loop (`add.f step, sScan` / `sne }2·4927+8, 2·4927` / `djn.f sAdd, <50302`) compares a pair 8 apart and steps 4,927 (coprime) at .67c, with a decrement stream. The `}` on the SNE ticks the A-field of every first cell it looks at.
2. The first find: `mov.b sScan, @sWip` copies the find's B-field into `sHead`'s, aiming a carpet three cells before it; `djn {sFlag, #45` counts the find and moves `sFlag`'s jump one line up. The loop now starts at `sWip`, which lays one `spl #4927, 4927` a lap at the carpet pointer: a blur scanner at .5c.
3. Each later find moves the jump up once more, to `sHead`, for one lap: `mov }sFlag, >1800` drops a copy of itself on a forward stream 1,800 cells on and its `}` puts the jump back.
4. After 45 finds the DJN falls through to the SPL that has served as increment and bomb; running it splits for ever and feeds a forward clear with `dat <1`.

**Why it is strong.** It scans at .67c until it has something, then never stops carpeting; the carpet bomb is also its step, so the code is 11 lines.

**New block: `arrow-scanner`.** The catalog's `blur-carpet` fails its own watch when booted (the frame bug listed in block-gaps.md), and booted `pair-wipe-scanner` as a stand-in gave 1.31. The new block is Arrow X's loop with the gate cells inside the block. Alone it scores 1.41 against Arrow X's 1.33 (20 rounds), 49-47-4 head to head. It carpets its own scan loop when a find lands among its own cells, as Arrow X does; the watch allows that and refuses only a carpet on the clear.

**Fidelity.** 1.56 / 1.60, 39-38-23.

**ModelWar.** 1.73. Above Decoy Adapter v6.1 against Drift v2 (2.12), Drift (2.13), clu_3 (1.82) and NovaBomber (2.89 / 2.81). With `switch-score` (WIN 1, LOSS -1, STREAK 3) in place of the table: 1.74 on ModelWar and 1.56 on the hill, the same as the table within noise. The arms alone on ModelWar: the airbag stone 1.62 (2.21 against clu_3 and Drift v2, 0.21 against Mercenary), arrow-scanner 1.62.

**Lessons.** The best scanner arm for this frame is the cheapest: an 11-line blur scanner beside Moore's stone is within 0.04 of the original.

## 5. KAT v5 (Dave Hillis)

**What it is.** A P^3 switcher with a 15-row table over five warriors: Katafutr's silk-imp paper (w0), Katafutr's incendiary bomber (w1), Katafutr's three-way scanner (w2), Robert Macrae's 55HSB (w4) and Denial (w5).

**How it works.**

1. *Brain.* The same five-line dispatcher as #2 with moduli 13 (loss), 14 (win) and 15 (tie). Row 0 falls through to row 1, so the first round plays 55HSB; its stored number 1,698 sends a loss to row 8 (55HSB again), a win to row 4 (the scanner) and a tie to row 3 (the paper). Rows by arm: 55HSB 1, 8, 9; Denial 2, 11, 12, 13; paper 3, 10; bomber 6, 7; scanner 4, 5, 14. Each arm's rows chain into each other on wins and ties and hand over on losses; the table is in the source's comments and was not probed row by row.
2. *55HSB.* Two pointers 26,700 apart advance by 12; a five-instruction loop compares the pair and tests each cell (.8c); a find is wiped forward with SPL until the first empty cell; after 13 self-scans it carpets the core backward with `stp >0, #0` and then runs a DAT clear stepping by the 13-point imp step.
3. *Katafutr scanner.* Boots two more copies a third of the core apart (one process each); each is a .5c F-scan (step 13) confined to its third by `mod #18470`; a find is wiped with SPL until empty, then it tries the cell the find's field points to (spiral search against imps); 9 self-scans, or a non-zero gate cell, lead to a DAT clear.
4. *Incendiary bomber.* Six processes booted 9,362 away enter a five-line loop one line apart; each pass writes an SPL, an incendiary `mov -16134, }-16134` and a copy, stepping 3·16,134; `jmn.a` watches a planted check cell 23 cells on and drops into a SPL-then-DAT clear when it reads zero.
5. *Paper.* Two launchers (`eStart`, `eBackup`) each make 18 processes and run two silk stages (16,211, 21,445) ending in a continuous 17-point pump.
6. *Denial.* As #8.

**Why it is strong.** Five strategies cover each other: 55HSB first (anti-imp), Denial against scanners, the paper and bomber against stones.

**Rebuild.** `switch-score` over the five arms (55HSB first, STREAK 3): booted `silk-imps` (17 points, the two steps), booted `incendiary`, booted `follow-scanner` (step 13) for the three-way scanner, booted `brainwash-scanner` (WASH 1) for 55HSB, and `boot(stone-dual-pump)`. 199 cells. `switch-table` cannot hold five arms or 15 states, so a score-box selector stands in.

**Fidelity.** 1.09 / 1.39, 24-50-26. The arms alone (20 rounds, rebuild / original): paper 0.46 / 1.23, bomber 0.47 / 1.31, scanner 0.78 / 0.59, 55HSB 0.45 / 0.78, Denial 1.55 / 1.51. Three of the five stand-ins are far weaker than the originals; the scanner stand-in is better.

**ModelWar.** 1.58. Above Decoy Adapter v6.1 against Drift v2 (2.00 / 1.76) and level with it against PROBE (2.74 / 2.73) and DriftLab (2.27 / 2.25).

**Lessons.** Katafutr's paper and bomber score 1.23 and 1.31 alone on this hill, not far below Denial's stone/imp (1.51); our `silk-imps` and `incendiary` score under 0.5. Those two (an 18-process silk ending in a continuous pump, and a six-process three-write incendiary bomber with a check cell) are the clearest missing blocks this pass found.

## 6. Azathoth (John Metcalf)

**What it is.** A 22-line paper/stone with no P-space.

**How it works.** An 11-line fan boot: `spl 2 / spl 1 / spl 1` makes six lockstep processes; `mov <p, {p` and `mov <stone-1, {s` copy the paper 6,172 on and the stone 5,770 back, one cell each; five SPLs through the stone vector send 30 processes into the stone and `djn.f` sends the six into the paper. The stone (`spl #0` / `mov bomb, @ptr` / `sub.x #2·23431, @stone` / `mov {-23878, }first` / `djn.f @hit, }ptr` / `dat <23431, >1`) bombs every 2·23,431 (shares 2 with the core), writes through the A-field of the next target, and adds a decrement stream; after 10,230 passes its bomb lands on its own SUB and the loop becomes `spl #0 / mov bomb, @ptr / dat >1`, a clear that moves one cell for every process that dies. The paper copies itself 16,342 on each lap (the distance growing as its A-postincrement pointers run), checks the cell after the new copy and drops a copy that was hit. Stone and paper share one queue, so the stone runs faster only while the paper is stunned.

**Rebuild.** `runner-paper-stone` (K 5) with the original's constants. 23 cells.

**Fidelity.** 1.48 / 1.48, 5-2-93: the same warrior in effect.

**ModelWar.** 1.30 with the constants scaled from 55,440, against 1.42 for the lead's hand-tuned port and 1.69 for runner-paper-stone's ModelWar-evolved defaults: the 55,440 numbers do not carry over. It beats Decoy Adapter v6.1 against Skippy (3.00), DriftLab (2.54) and PROBE (2.87) and scores about 1.0 (ties) against everything else.

**Lessons.** A process-sharing paper/stone in 22 lines ranks sixth among 200-cell switchers; the existing block reproduces it exactly.

## 7. Void Runner (John Metcalf)

**What it is.** A 25-line stone/imp with no P-space.

**How it works.** A 13-line fan boot: two `spl 1` make four processes that copy three imp pumps (to iboot and iboot ± 18,480, a third of the core apart), one more `spl 1` makes eight that copy the eight-cell stone 25,256 away, two more make 32; three SPLs and a final `djn.f` send 32 processes into each of the four bodies. The stone is Azathoth's engine with step 8,807 and 12,696 passes, ending as the one-cell-per-process clear (about 50,700 cycles alone). Each pump is `spl #0 / add.f #32191, launch / djn.f imp-32191·32, <3455` over `mov.i #32191, *0`: it launches one 31-point imp a pass at the next point of the spiral, the first 32 points behind the template so the 32 entrants line up.

**A finding.** The original's stone bombs its own first pump: the model of its bomb sequence puts pass 3,406 on the pump's DJN line, and alone in the VM that line is a stone bomb by cycle 30,000 (and a second pump's line is dead by 45,000). The stone shuts its pumps down before it turns into a clear. Whether this is designed is **uncertain**.

**Rebuild.** `runner-stone-imp` (D 2, three pumps). It refuses a stone that bombs a pump, so the rebuild steps 8,773 with its trail at 3,000 (no search found another step accepted with the original's 12,696 passes). 25 cells.

**Fidelity.** 1.45 / 1.51, 0-0-100. With the original step and TIME cut to 4,650 (the longest the check allows): 1.38.

**ModelWar.** The scaled constants bomb a pump there too; the recipe uses runner-stone-imp's ModelWar-evolved defaults, measured at 1.67 by the lead.

**Lessons.** Letting the stone retire its own pumps late is part of how Void Runner works; a block contract that forbids any pump hit before the clear can't build the original.

## 8. Denial (David Moore)

**What it is.** A 196-cell stone/imp (24 live lines, the rest blank), no P-space; also an arm of KAT v5 and Big I.F.F.S.

**How it works.**

1. *Boot.* Three MOVs plant the stone's bomb and the two imp templates; `spl 2 / spl 2 / spl 1` make five lockstep processes; `mov {hMain, {away` copies the five-line stone (hMain's A-field counts) and `mov <iSrc, <away` the four-line pump and a blank (iSrc's B-field counts); two `spl 1` make 20 workers; `spl` into the stone, `spl @away` into the pump and `jmp *away` into the stone give 40 processes to the stone and 20 to the pump (measured).
2. *Stone.* `spl #5, <djn` heads the loop and is re-entered every pass, so the stone keeps splitting; `mov bomb, @hPtr` writes `dat 859, >1` at X; `add #1718, hPtr`; `mov bomb, }ptr` writes 1,718 on, or through the A-field of a cell already holding a bomb, 859 on; `djn.f` adds a decrement stream. 859 is coprime with the core. After 11,650 passes the bomb lands on its own ADD and it becomes the one-cell-per-process clear.
3. *Pump.* `spl #29179, <34117` (it splits too; its fields are the two ring steps, and its B operand is a decrement stream at the 13-point step), `add.f -1, 1` steps both launch vectors, `spl` sends a process to the next point of a 19-point spiral of B-imps (`mov.i #5, 29179`), `jmp @-1` sends this one to the next point of a 13-point spiral of A-imps (`mov.i #34117, *0`). The vectors start 20 and 40 ring steps behind the templates, as many as the workers that step them before the first launch.

**A finding.** As in Void Runner, the stone bombs its own pump: in the VM the pump's SPL line is a DAT by cycle 60,000 (the bomb sequence puts it about 6,600 passes in), and its own DJN line later; with the DJN gone, the `spl #5` still sends one pass per process round, so the stone goes on.

**New block: `stone-dual-pump`.** `fanboot`, `dual-imp-pump` and the catalog stones can't share this boot (five lockstep processes copying two bodies, then two of every three into the stone). The block is Denial's engine with its numbers as defaults; its contract refuses a stone that hits its own SPL, MOV, ADD or pointer line before the time-up and one that hits the pump in the first 1,000 passes, and lets the late pump hit through.

**Fidelity.** 1.48 / 1.56, 0-1-99.

**ModelWar.** 1.54. Above Decoy Adapter v6.1 only against Skippy (2.98 / 2.73) and DriftLab (2.69 / 2.25); a tie machine against most of the field.

**Lessons.** Two imp rings of different sizes, with the step in different fields, from one four-line loop; a bomb or gate tuned to one ring leaves the other. The 170 blank cells only spread the source image.

## 9. Big I.F.F.S. (Dave Hillis)

**What it is.** A 200-cell switcher over four of KAT's arms (bomber, Katafutr scanner, 55HSB, Denial; 8 of 15 rows are Denial), with a handshake in front.

**How it works.** A sleeper check first (P-cell 555 = 200 parks it in `jmp #0`). Then Macrae's Bigboy handshake over shared P-space (`PIN 5339`): after the first decided round each copy writes a win or loss flag and reads the other; a partner with the same PIN makes them throw alternate rounds; anyone else is marked enemy and from then on the P^3 table runs directly. A sanity cell (12,245) catches a brainwash and rebuilds P-space. The table's first round plays the Katafutr scanner. Components as #5 and #8.

**Rebuild.** `switch-score` (scanner first, STREAK 3) over booted `incendiary`, `follow-scanner`, `brainwash-scanner` and `stone-dual-pump`. The handshake needs P-space shared between two warriors, which the VM does not give (each warrior's P-space is private), and against any other warrior it only marks it as an enemy.

**Fidelity.** 1.22 / 1.24, 28-55-17. Close on the hill, beaten head to head; the bomber stand-in is the weak arm (as in #5).

**ModelWar.** 1.55. Above Decoy Adapter v6.1 against Drift v2 (2.13 / 1.76), Skippy (2.78 / 2.73) and DriftLab (2.43 / 2.25).

**Lessons.** A PIN handshake is for teams of entries on one hill; against everyone else it costs nothing but code.

## 10. Discord (John Metcalf)

**What it is.** A 16-line scanner, one process, no boot, no P-space.

**How it works.**

1. It enters at `ptrs`. The loop `sub inc, ptrs` / `sne fi-8, fi` / `sub inc, ptrs` / `seq *ptrs, @ptrs` / `slt #30, ptrs` / `djn.f scan, <-10000` compares two pairs eight apart every five instructions (.8c), stepping 8,286 (shares 6 with the core: 9,240 positions, one lap in about 23,100 cycles), with a decrement stream that starts 10,000 cells back. After a difference the SEQ re-tests the same pair, and the SLT ignores B-fields under 30 (its own cells).
2. A find: `mov.b ptrs, #0` sets the wipe pointer five cells past the find's B cell; eight passes of two `mov` write 16 SPLs (`spl #-8286, -8286`, also the step) downward over both cells of the pair; then `jmz scan, scan-1` returns to the scan while the cell before the scanner is empty.
3. *The end.* The pair pointers start at STEP - 1, so the B pointer always sits one below a multiple of the step from `ptrs`. At the end of its first lap the B pointer lands on `scan`, the cell just below `ptrs`: the pair differs (code against empty), the SLT does not catch a pointer just below itself, and the 16-cell wipe runs from three cells above `ptrs` down over the scan loop and the flag cell. The JMZ sees the flag and falls into `spl` (the increment, which now splits for ever) and a d-clear running on the wipe line's fields (`dat 1, 9`; the bomb's 9 resets the pointer past the code). Alone, its first find is its own stream's edge at cycle 1,988 and the clear starts at cycle 23,552 (measured). The scanner wipes its own scan loop on purpose: that loop has nothing left to do.

**Why it is strong.** It scans at .8c for one lap and then clears, with no timer and no counter: the first lap's self-find is the switch.

**New block: `flag-cmp-scanner`.** `pair-wipe-scanner` with Discord's numbers scored 0.47. The new block is Discord's engine. Its first build started the pair pointers 26 cells further on; the self-find then never hit the head, the scanner never reached its clear, ran until its own stream reached its DJN, and scored **0.41** (2-95-3 against the original). With the first look at STEP - 1 it scores 1.32. The watch allows the end-of-lap wipe over the scan loop and refuses one over the wipe, the clear or the bomb.

**Fidelity.** 1.32 / 1.35, 45-50-5.

**ModelWar.** 1.71 (step 3,774, stream 4,545), the best single rebuild there. Above Decoy Adapter v6.1 against Drift v2 (2.60 / 1.76), Mercenary (1.78 / 1.60), InnovationStation (1.96 / 1.54) and NovaBomber (2.96 / 2.81); far below it against PROBE, Skippy and DriftLab (1.16 to 1.39 against 2.25 to 2.73).

**Lessons.** A scanner's endgame can be timed by geometry instead of a counter: one constant (the first look) decides whether Discord is a top-10 warrior or a 0.41 one.

## Hill summary

**Recurring patterns.**

- *Moore's airbag stone is the hill's backbone.* It is an arm of #1 to #4, and alone it outscores all four. Its strength is the MOV bomb (enemy processes keep copying bombs instead of dying, so a stunned opponent wastes its turns), the exact time-up through its own pointer line, and a tiny booted footprint.
- *Switchers are frames.* #2, #3 and #4 share brain and stone byte for byte and differ only in the scanner; #5 and #9 share four arms. The brain decides little on this hill: the best switcher arm alone beats every switcher, because the hill's opponents (mostly without P-space) do not adapt.
- *Stones that retire their own pumps.* Void Runner's and Denial's stones bomb their own imp pumps before they turn into clears; the contracts of our stone/imp blocks forbid that, so the originals can't be built exactly.
- *Endgames triggered by the warrior's own geometry.* Moore's stone ends when its lattice comes home; Discord when its first lap reaches its own head; Metcalf's stones when the bomb reaches their own SUB/ADD. None of them counts.
- *Footprint.* Boots that leave only live cells in the far core (Moore) beat blocks booted whole (1.56 against 1.40 for the same stone).

**What the catalog still can't express.**

- P^3 tables with more than four states or arms (Moore's six states, KAT's and Big I.F.F.S.'s 15 rows over four or five arms). `switch-table` takes four of each; a `ROWS` mode or a table combinator would cover all of them.
- A self-recognition handshake that throws rounds (the gate forbids self-kills) and a PIN handshake (the VM has no shared P-space).
- Katafutr's 18-process silk with a continuous 17-point pump, Katafutr's six-process incendiary bomber with its check cell, and 55HSB's two-pointer .8c scan: the stand-ins score 0.45 to 0.47 against 0.78 to 1.31 for the originals.
- Truth II and Recon 2 exactly: `follow-scanner` scores 0.59 against Truth II's 0.86.
- The gate counts a switch's own dispatch code as live after it has dispatched, and counts the boot source as live whenever one member is not under a boot. A tree that mixes a self-booting block with unbooted members fails even when nothing live is hit; wrapping every arm in `boot` is the workaround used here.

**What transfers to ModelWar.**

- No rebuild reaches Decoy Adapter v6.1's 2.16; the best are Ballista (1.73), Fire and Brimstone (1.72) and Discord (1.71), above runner-stone-imp (1.67).
- Against single opponents several beat it: Discord against Drift v2 (2.60 / 1.76), InnovationStation (1.96 / 1.54), Mercenary (1.78 / 1.60) and NovaBomber (2.96 / 2.81); the Moore switchers against Drift v2, Drift and clu_3; Denial against Skippy and DriftLab. Discord's engine is the best candidate for a new arm: it covers exactly the opponents where Decoy Adapter is weakest (Drift v2, Mercenary, InnovationStation).
- Selectors: Ballista's arms under Moore's table (four states) score 1.73 on ModelWar and under `switch-score` 1.74; on the hill 1.56 and 1.56. Neither selector beats the other, and no idea in these five brains (the P^3 table, score boxes with a draw streak, handshakes) would beat `switch-score` as it stands. What the switchers gain over their best arm comes from the arms, not the brain: the airbag stone alone scores 1.62 on ModelWar, Ballista 1.73.

The numbers above are single runs with seed 1 on each hill; where a statement rests on reading code rather than a run, it says so.
