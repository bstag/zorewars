# Koenigstuhl '88 hill, top 10: rebuilt from blocks

The ten warriors at the top of the Koenigstuhl ICWS'88 hill (core 8000, no P-space, strict '88 syntax), each read, traced in the VM, rebuilt as a block tree and measured against its original. Line-by-line source notes for these warriors are in [../hill-top30/88.md](../hill-top30/88.md); this document is about how they work in play, how close the rebuilds come, and what they teach.

Recipes: `tools/algebra/recipes-top10/88.js` (keys `top10-88-<name>`). New blocks: `tools/algebra/blocks/top10-88.js`. Tests: `test/algebra-top10-88.test.js`. Build any of them with `node scripts/algebra.js recipe top10-88-talisman --rules 88 --length 100 --out x.red`.

## How it was measured

- **Hill score**: points per round against the hill's top 30, 100 rounds per opponent (`hill <x> --hill koenigstuhl:88 --top 30 --rules 88 --length 100 --rounds 100`). The top 30 includes each original, so an original plays itself once.
- **Body**: the same with the quickscan cut away. For the originals this was done on a local scratch copy by pointing `END` at the boot (Freight Train has a switch for it); for the rebuilds, by building the body block alone. Comparing bodies separates the two halves of each design.
- **Head-to-head**: rebuild against original, 200 rounds, wins-losses-ties for the rebuild. In a near-mirror most rounds tie; equal strength shows as equal wins and losses.
- **ModelWar**: the 17-warrior ModelWar field ('94, 25,200 cells, P-space), 100 rounds each way per opponent, built with `--rules modelwar --length 200` (the blocks size their safety margins from the length limit, and 5040 would refuse every hop on a 25,200 core; the images are 68 to 100 cells). Constants were rescaled by about 3.15 and steps chosen with the same gcd structure. 3- and 7-point imp rings do not exist on 25,200 cells (it is divisible by 3 and 7), so the imp blocks take a point count and use 13 there. Reference: Decoy Adapter v6.1 scores 2.16 on this field, Trio v10 1.98, runner-stone-imp 1.67.

All numbers are from 22 November 2026, one seed set; differences under about 0.03 points are noise.

## The opening is where the points were hiding

Seven of the ten open with a Metcalf- or Janeczek-style quickscan (see the families in [../hill-top30/88.md](../hill-top30/88.md)). The first rebuilds used our existing '88 quickscans, and they made every warrior *worse* than its own body: Talisman's body scored 1.168, and with `opening-qscan-table-88` (18 pairs, its own burst) 1.157; Into the Unknown's body 1.217, with the quickscan 1.171. The originals' quickscans add 0.13 to 0.25.

Grafting Talisman's original quickscan text onto our body (a scratch file, not in the repo) gave 1.302, so the body was not the problem. Two cut-down grafts located the value:

| Talisman's quickscan on our body | Hill score |
|---|---|
| as written (18 pairs, 9-pass burst, bomb `DAT <-46, <46`) | 1.302 |
| burst cut to 1 pass | 1.123 (below no quickscan at all) |
| bomb changed to `DAT #0, #0` | 1.228 |

The scan finds; the **burst** earns the points, and its bomb's predecrements are worth 0.07. Then the early race: of 200 rounds against Talisman, the graft won 44 decided before cycle 300 and lost 40; with `opening-qscan-table-88` it won 28 and lost 45. Both sides run a quickscan, and whoever bombs first wins those rounds. Our quickscan decodes into a probe, tests the side, and hands to a separate burst; theirs adds the decoded offset straight into the burst instruction's own fields and starts bombing two cycles later.

`opening-qscan-m-88` does the same thing in its own encoding: the decoder ADDs land in the carpet MOV, whose B-field is the pointer, so the second write of each pass can aim through the bomb the first just laid (`MOV src, <ptr` decrements that bomb's B-field and writes DB-1 beyond it). With it, Talisman's rebuild scored 1.305, matching the graft; it wins 38 early rounds against the original and loses 44. On a stationary 40-cell target it kills in 16 of 400 rounds against 19 for the original and 10 for `opening-qscan-table-88`. The separate `probe-carpet-88` (the same carpet as an add-on to any probe) was kept as a block, but behind a separate quickscan it is too slow to help: about 1.14 to 1.17 on Talisman's body.

The exception is Mountain Viper, whose own 10-pair quickscan adds 0.12 but ours *costs* 0.01 to 0.06 (best 1.284 with a Metcalf-style burst, against 1.294 with no quickscan). Why is not established: the scan positions, the upward burst (`ADD #9` in the original) or how the 100-cell image is laid out are the candidates.

## 1. Lithobolia C yvfp (Steve Gunnell)

**What it is.** A 13-pair quickscan followed by a volley of seven resin stones and a three-stage paper whose copies end as imp pumps. 99 instructions.

**How it works.**
1. *Opening.* Metcalf's quickscan cut to 13 pairs, with very wide pairs (cells 1168 apart, pairs 2336 apart) so the 26 cells cover the core in bigger strides, and a 12-pass burst stepping 5 with `DAT <-48, <48`.
2. *Launch.* An unbalanced SPL tree (`spl 24 / spl 12 / spl 6 ...`) gives eight leaves within a few cycles. Seven leaves are five lines each: four MOVs copy the four-line resin through the B-field of the leaf's own `JMP @0`, which then points at the copy and jumps into it. Resins land 992 apart starting 6247 past the image. The eighth leaf makes six processes for the paper.
3. *Resin.* `SPL 0 / MOV C, <C / MOV <C, <-661 / DJN A, <stream`: the loop lays copies of its drop line (`MOV <0, <p`) downward, two cells a pass, starting 661 below. The DJN returns to the SPL, so the processes double every few passes. A drop is not a DAT: a looping enemy whose jump is replaced walks off its own end; one held by an SPL drags empty cells down onto itself (traced in `resin-stone`).
4. *Paper.* Six processes copy the 12-line paper 2302 away, then the last six lines 2949 away, then 4368 away; every copy finishes in `SPL 0 / ADD #2667, 1 / JMP @0, ...` over one imp, a 3-point pump.

**Why it is strong.** Seven independent resins cannot all be found by a scanner, each grows to fill the process table, and the paper keeps launching imps, so it ties what it cannot kill (1444 of 3000 rounds were ties).

**Rebuild.** `seq(opening-qscan-m-88 {PAIRS 13, GAP 1168, COUNT 12, STEP 5, DB 48}, resin-paper-88)`. The new block builds the SPL tree over RESINS+1 leaves, copies the resin template per leaf exactly as above, and runs a straight-line paper with a pump tail. SPLIT 1 (loop back to the SPL) was worth 0.10 on the body against looping to the layer line.

**Fidelity.** Hill 1.210 against 1.363; body 1.053 against 1.203; head-to-head 37-35-128 (level). The gap is in the body. The paper differs: Lithobolia runs six processes over 12-, 6- and 6-cell copy windows, the block runs LEN processes over LEN-cell windows. Heads, starting processes, stages and stream position were tried and none closed it.

**ModelWar.** Not built: the pump tail is fixed at 3 points.

**Lessons.** Many small growing bodies are hard to kill but rarely kill: Lithobolia's strength is its tie rate. Its resins ignore their own drops landing on each other, which is why so many can share the core.

## 2. Talisman (John Metcalf)

**What it is.** The full 18-pair Metcalf quickscan, then the classic '88 stone/imp: a pair-bombing stone on four processes and a one-process 3-point imp pump. 69 instructions.

**How it works.**
1. *Opening.* 18 pairs, 130 apart, spread every 260 cells from about 520 past the code; a decoded hit gets a 9-pass carpet of `DAT <-46, <46` stepping 7, two writes a pass (see above).
2. *Boot.* Three MOVs lay three imp instructions at the spot the pump's first launches will hit; one MOV places the stone's bomb 16 cells past the stone copy; `SPL 2 / SPL 2 / SPL 1` make five processes, each copying one launcher cell and one stone cell; `DJN @sboot, #5` sends four into the stone and the fifth falls through into the pump.
3. *Stone.* `SPL 0 / MOV bomb, <ptr / MOV bomb, @stone / SUB #2211, @hit / DJN stone, <2438`. Each pass bombs the pointer cell and the cell below it, the pointer moving 2211 a pass; the SPL head adds a process each pass, so the stone speeds up as it runs.
4. *The end of the stone.* The pointer starts at 1 + 1819 x 2211, so after pass 1819 (about cycle 8,700) it points at the second MOV, which then bombs itself. Traced: from then on every process the SPL makes runs the first MOV once and dies on the new DAT, and that MOV's `<1` walks the DAT's B-field down, so the stone has become `SPL 0 / MOV bomb, <1 / DAT`: an SPL-fed backward clear with `DAT <-19, <-19` bombs. By cycle 70,000 the core is full and about 20 imps are left.
5. *Pump.* `SPL 0 / ADD #2668, iptr / JMP @0, ...`: every pass one more imp, each a ring step and a cell beyond the last, starting on the three seeds.

**Why it is strong.** The stone kills scanners and papers early, the clear finishes what is left, and the imps make the losses into ties. No instruction is spent on the timer: the self-hit is the timer.

**Rebuild.** `seq(opening-qscan-m-88, selfclear-stone-pump-88 {STEP 5789})`. The new block is this boot, stone and pump; TIME sets the pass of the self-hit (the pointer starts at 1 - TIME x STEP), and the contract refuses any earlier pass that would hit the stone's head, MOVs or ADD, or the pump. A hit on the DJN is allowed: the SPL keeps a loop-less stone running (Quicksilver lives through exactly that).

**Fidelity.** Hill 1.305 against 1.346; body 1.168 against 1.194; head-to-head 45-55-100.

**ModelWar.** 1.269 (13-point pump, step 18205, TIME 3450). It beats Decoy Adapter v6.1 against no opponent; its best are Skippy (2.71) and PROBE (2.52).

**Lessons.** A stone that ends by hitting itself and turns into a clear is the cheapest phase change there is. The rebuild needed one cell more than the original (71 against 69) and no counter.

## 3. Freight Train v0.2 (David Moore)

**What it is.** Moore's q^2 quickscan, a Cannonade-style stone and a 7-point imp spiral fed by two continuous pumps. 74 instructions.

**How it works.**
1. *Opening.* q^2: 13 pairs with decoder tables kept in the B-fields of the launcher's own SPL lines; a hit gets a stun pit (`SPL 0 / SPL -1 / JMP -2`) and four passes of JMP bombs.
2. *Boot.* `main` forks the launch branch, makes four processes that copy the four-line stone and two copies of the three-line pump (the copy counters are the fan lines' B-fields), and sends all four into the stone at its SPL.
3. *Launch.* Eight processes hit `SPL 2`: eight run `JMP @0, imp-is`, eight run `ADD #is, -1` between them, so eight imps land on consecutive points of a 7-point spiral (step 1143), the eighth one lap and a cell on. The adders then jump into the stone (twelve stone processes). A third branch lays the seed imp, places the stone's end bomb `DAT <2667, <1` seven cells past the stone, starts pump 1, aims pump 2 and becomes it.
4. *Stone.* `MOV <src, tgt / SPL -1, <.. / ADD 1, -2 / DJN -2, <..`. The SPL forks a MOV every pass; the ADD adds the DJN's own fields (-2, -82) to the MOV, so the source walks down two cells and the target 82. The MOV copies whatever is under a decrement stream (mostly empty cells: DAT bombs). After 2437 passes the source reaches the end bomb and the target reaches the DJN: the DJN becomes `DAT <2667, <1`, the ADD now adds that bomb's fields (2667, 1), and the stone is a forward clear one cell a pass whose source jumps 2667 a pass, decrementing cells a 3-point imp step apart. Traced: the switch happens between cycles 9,000 and 12,000.
5. *Pumps.* `SPL 0 / ADD #1144, 1 / JMP @0, ...` twice, launching into the spiral's growing front. Alone it holds about 39 imps at cycle 20,000.

**Why it is strong.** Twelve-plus stone processes that never stop forking, a clear with anti-imp side effects, and a 7-point spiral: the 3-point anti-imp bombs elsewhere on the hill miss it.

**Rebuild.** `seq(opening-qscan-m-88 {PAIRS 13, GAP 163}, cannonade-pump-88)`. The new block is the whole body. The pumps' first launch offsets (O1, O2) were found by running it alone and counting imps (33 to 34 at cycle 20,000 against the original's 39); deriving them exactly from the timing was not done. Moore's q^2 itself has no block: `opening-q2-stun-88` (a linear decoder, at most 10 pairs) in its place scored 1.210, worse than no quickscan (1.241), so the recipe uses `opening-qscan-m-88`.

**Fidelity.** Hill 1.366 against 1.382; body 1.241 against 1.270; head-to-head 33-15-152.

**ModelWar.** 0.949 (13-point spiral, step 246, TIME 3450). The pump offsets were not re-tuned for 13 points, so the pumps probably do not feed the spiral there (uncertain).

**Lessons.** A stone can carry its own clear as data: one bomb placed beside it, reached by its source pointer at a chosen pass, rewires the loop. Feeding a spiral needs its launch offsets to match the spiral's timing to the cell.

## 4. Resinoid (John Metcalf)

**What it is.** The 18-pair Metcalf quickscan, two resin clears 4000 apart and a paper that grows imps. 68 instructions.

**How it works.**
1. *Boot.* `SPL 2 / SPL 1 / SPL 1` make six processes; each copies one cell of the resin to both resin sites and one cell of the paper; then `SPL @0 / SPL @0 / JMP @0` put six processes in each resin and six in the paper.
2. *Resin.* Two SPL 0 heads over the three-line drop-laying loop (`MOV 1, <1 / MOV <0, <3321 / DJN clear, <stream`), looping to the layer line.
3. *Paper.* `MOV <6, <papa / SPL @0, 1085+6`: one copy stage, then `SPL 0 / SPL 2668 / MOV -1, 2667 / MOV 0, 2667` — the processes split, launch onto cells a ring step on, and copy the launcher line and the imp ahead, building 3-point imp chains in place. Exactly how the chains close into rings was not traced (uncertain).

**Why it is strong.** As Lithobolia: resins that grow and do not die, plus imps for ties (1456 of 3000 ties).

**Rebuild.** `seq(opening-qscan-m-88, resin-paper-88 {RESINS 2, HEADS 2, SPLIT 0, HOP 4000, RPROCS 6, STAGES 1, ...})`. RPROCS adds `SPL @` lines so each resin starts with six processes (+0.08 on the body against one); the tail is the 3-point pump, not Resinoid's in-place maker.

**Fidelity.** Hill 1.146 against 1.311; body 1.042 against 1.182; head-to-head 35-37-128. The remaining gap is most likely the imp tail.

**ModelWar.** Not built (3-point pump tail).

**Lessons.** Starting a growing body with more processes matters more than its shape: six starting processes per resin were worth 0.08.

## 5. Quicksilver '88 (Michal Janeczek)

**What it is.** A 12-pair Janeczek quickscan and the smallest stone/imp on the hill (19 instructions of body). 55 instructions.

**How it works.**
1. *Opening.* 12 pairs, 161 apart; the burst bomb is `JMP boot, <43`, which doubles as the quickscan's exit line.
2. *Boot.* Three processes lay three imps and copy the three-line pump, `MOV -3, 0` turns itself into an `SPL 1` so the count becomes five, five stone cells are copied, and a DJN sends four into the stone and the last to the pump.
3. *Stone.* Talisman's shape with step -107 and bomb `DAT <1, #17`.
4. *What actually happens* (traced; Michal's own comment says he saw "weird results"): at pass 47, cycle 339, the stone bombs its own pump head. The pump has launched about a dozen imps by then and launches nothing more. Around cycle 5,500 the stone bombs its own DJN; processes then die there, but the SPL keeps feeding a loop-less stone that still bombs. At pass 1868 it bombs its second MOV and becomes the same SPL-fed backward clear as Talisman's. By cycle 56,000 the core is full and 12 imps remain.

**Why it is strong.** Even with its pump dead, a dozen imps on a 3-point ring tie most of what the stone and clear do not kill, and the whole thing is small.

**Rebuild.** `seq(opening-qscan-m-88 {12 pairs, GAP 161, 6 passes, STEP 9}, selfclear-stone-pump-88 {STEP -107, TIME 1868, GUARD 0, DEC 17, ...})`. GUARD 0 lets the stone bomb its pump as the original does. The bomb's fields mattered: `DAT <17, <17` scored 1.189 on the body, `DAT <1, <1` 0.97 and `DAT <-19, <-19` 0.995.

**Fidelity.** Hill 1.266 against 1.303; body 1.189 against 1.184; head-to-head 24-32-144.

**ModelWar.** Not measured separately (the Talisman build is the same block).

**Lessons.** A stone fed by an SPL survives losing its own loop instruction. And an "accident" can be load-bearing: the rebuild only matched once it reproduced the pump being bombed.

## 6. Into the Unknown (John Metcalf)

**What it is.** The 18-pair Metcalf quickscan, then one boot that splits two to one between a timed stone and a paper. 73 instructions.

**How it works.**
1. *Boot.* The bomb is placed beside the stone copy; `SPL 2 / SPL 2 / SPL 1` make five processes that copy the five-line stone; `MOV -2, 0` overwrites itself with the `SPL 1` two lines up, so the first process passes and the other four split: nine processes, which copy the nine-line paper with the same source pointer; `SPL @sdest / SPL @0 / JMP @0` send 18 into the stone and 9 into the paper.
2. *Stone.* Talisman's stone with step -2211 (5789), `time 1344`, bomb `DAT <2667, <-6`.
3. *Paper.* Three `MOV <9, <p / SPL @0, step` stages (5656, 3152, 3408) and a tail `SPL 0 / MOV 0, <1 / DJN -1, #-80`, an SPL-fed clear from 80 below.
4. Traced alone: the paper's clears reach the stone and overwrite it between cycles 12,000 and 16,000, before the stone's own end at pass 1344. Against an opponent the stone's timing may matter more (not traced).

**Why it is strong.** Stone and paper together beat both scanners (the paper) and papers (the stone), and the 2:1 split costs only three instructions.

**Rebuild.** `seq(opening-qscan-m-88, paper-stone-boot-88)`. The new block keeps the 5-to-9 doubling trick and the 18/9 split.

**Fidelity.** Hill 1.292 against 1.356; body 1.217 against 1.189; head-to-head 41-58-101.

**ModelWar.** 1.113. Above Decoy Adapter against Skippy (2.87 vs 2.73) and DriftLab (2.56 vs 2.25).

**Lessons.** `MOV -2, 0` in the middle of a fan turns n processes into 2n-1 at the point where the next copy needs them; one boot can serve two bodies of different sizes.

## 7. Mountain Viper (inversed)

**What it is.** A 10-pair Janeczek quickscan, a booted one-shot scanner and a 42-cell SPL decoy. 100 instructions.

**How it works.**
1. *Opening.* 10 pairs, 624 apart, 12-pass burst stepping 9 upward with `DAT <40, <-39`.
2. *Boot.* Copies the five-line scanner 5828 away and the six-line clear STEP+2 past it, starts the scanner, lays the kill DAT nine cells below the clear and dies on its own `DAT`: nothing live stays in the image.
3. *Scanner.* `SUB inc, look / CMP a+12, a / JMP 23, 23 / DJN scan, @look`: three cycles for two cells. The JMP line is both the increment (its fields are 23, 23) and the jump into the clear, which sits exactly 23 cells after it. The DJN also decrements every scanned B-field.
4. *Clear.* `MOV look, @cMov1` copies the scan line, with the find in its fields, into the clear's pointer cell; then `SPL 0, <-8 / MOV @cref, <ptr / MOV @cref, <ptr / DJN` lays SPL 0 downward from beside the find. When the clear comes round to its own pointer cell it writes the SPL there; the pointer's B-field becomes -8, the next write skips the clear and lands on `cref`, which now aims at the kill DAT: the SPL pass is followed by a DAT pass. Traced: alone the core is full by cycle 20,000 and the warrior ends as `SPL 0 / DAT <-9, #0`, a two-process gate.

**Why it is strong.** It is the best scanner on the hill: it wins or loses almost every round (439 ties in 3000), and the decoy makes enemy one-shots trigger on nothing.

**Rebuild.** `image(seq(opening-qscan-m-88 {10 pairs, GAP 624}, oneshot-88), spl-decoy-strip-88 {42, CYCLE 7})`. Alone the rebuilt body's process counts match the original's at every 10,000-cycle checkpoint.

**Fidelity.** Hill 1.284 against 1.420; body with decoy 1.294 against 1.300; head-to-head 87-113-0. The gap is the quickscan (above).

**ModelWar.** 1.536, the best rebuild there. It beats Decoy Adapter v6.1 against clu_3 (2.01 vs 1.73), Drift_v2 (2.77 vs 1.76) and NovaBomber (2.93 vs 2.81).

**Lessons.** One line can be the increment and the jump; the clear's pointer can be the scan line itself; the pass switch can be the pointer overwriting itself. Mountain Viper spends no instruction on any of those transitions.

## 8. Zygote, hill version (Roy van Rijn)

**What it is.** A 12-pair quickscan, a stone and a looping paper that run where they were loaded (no boot), and 34 empty cells. 100 instructions.

**How it works.**
1. *Start.* Seven `SPL stone` lines put seven processes in the stone; `SPL 1 / SPL 1 / MOV -1, 0` make seven for the paper.
2. *Paper* (traced): `ADD #7, ptr / MOV <-1, <1 / SPL @0, 7349 / MOV bomb, <-1 / MOV bomb, <ptr / JMZ paper, paper / bomb DAT <2667, <5334`. The seven processes run in lockstep. The ADD's B-field is the copy counter: seven predecrements count it from 7 to 0 while copying the seven cells; seven SPLs start seven processes in the copy; seven bombs go on the cells right under the copy; seven more go down a stream. The JMZ sees the counter at 0 and loops; next time round the first ADD lands on itself and re-arms the counter to 7 while the other six move the stream on. Each loop's copy lands 14 cells below the last.
3. *Stone.* Quicksilver's stone (step -107) on seven processes.

**Why it is strong.** Nothing is booted, so the first thing a scanner meets is the quickscan or empty padding; the paper lays a carpet of copies and anti-imp bombs.

**Rebuild.** `image(seq(opening-qscan-m-88 {12 pairs}, stone-loop-paper-88), blank-padding-88 {33})` (33, not 34: our quickscan is one cell longer).

**Fidelity.** Hill 1.231 against 1.329; body 1.145 against 1.075 (better); head-to-head 41-67-92. Zygote's quickscan adds 0.25 to its body, more than any other here; ours adds 0.09.

**ModelWar.** 1.091; above Decoy Adapter against PROBE (2.89 vs 2.73), Skippy (2.92 vs 2.73) and DriftLab (2.63 vs 2.25).

**Lessons.** A counter can re-arm itself: the ADD that loops lands on its own B-field once per loop. Lockstep processes turn a one-cell instruction into a seven-cell operation.

## 9. Elemental Dust 2 (John Metcalf)

**What it is.** No quickscan: a vampire with a self-clearing pit, a 3-point imp ring and decoys in every free cell. 100 instructions, 26 of them code.

**How it works.**
1. *Boot.* Copies the vampire, its fang, its increment and the four-line pit 3054 away, keeping their spacing; starts the vampire; lays one imp 6619 away; a binary SPL tree sends six processes to `imp + k*2667`, k = 0..5 (two laps of a 3-point ring), and one more to the vampire.
2. *Vampire.* `SPL 0, <-14 / MOV fang, @fang / ADD inc, @vloop / DJN vloop, <stream`: SPL-fed like a stone. The fang `JMP` keeps A+B equal to the distance to the pit while the increment moves it 644 a pass.
3. *Pit.* `MOV bomb, <ptr / SPL pit / JMP @pit+1`: each prisoner splits and lays `DAT <2667, ..` downward, clearing the core for the owner with anti-imp bombs.
4. Side effects (fang 86 computed from the step, the second seen in the gate run): fang 86 lands on the pit's own JMP (harmless: it is a jump into the pit too), and after about 2000 fangs the drop comes round to the vampire's own loop line, so the vampire's processes jump into the pit and join the clear. Alone, by the end, the clear has also gone over the imps.
5. *Decoys.* Every other cell is `DAT #pit, #1`; why that form was chosen is not established (uncertain).

**Why it is strong.** Captured processes do the clearing, so a stronger opponent clears for it; the decoys and a small real body make it hard to find.

**Rebuild.** `pit-vampire-imp-88`: one block with the same layout (fang 16, pit 27, increment 40 from the vampire head) and the decoys in the gaps. Its early timeline alone (process and imp counts at cycles 500 to 3000) matches the original's exactly.

**Fidelity.** Hill 1.350 against 1.359; head-to-head 78-59-63.

**ModelWar.** Not built: the launch tree is two laps of a 3-point ring.

**Lessons.** A vampire is a stone whose bombs work for it. Its self-capture at the end is a phase change for free.

## 10. Der Zweite Blitzkrieg 2021 (Mike Nonemacher)

**What it is.** The 18-pair Metcalf quickscan grafted onto Blitzkrieg-style stone/imp: a mod-8 stone that becomes a two-pass clear and a 15-imp spiral. 75 instructions.

**How it works.**
1. *Boot.* The first process forks the imp branch, lays the clear's gate, a spare `SPL 0` and the clear's pointer value at the copy site, makes six processes and copies the six-line stone/clear 3405 away.
2. *Stone.* `SPL 0, <-19 / SUB inc, 1 / MOV <-19, 1 / JMP -2, <..` with the increment `DAT #328, #-328` booted 20 cells off. Each pass the MOV copies the cell under a decrement stream onto a cell 328 further on: targets on one residue mod 8, 1000 of them.
3. *Its end.* After 1000 passes the target is back on its own JMP and the source on the spare SPL: the JMP becomes `SPL 0`, the processes fall through into `MOV @PTR, <inc / JMP -1`, a clear whose source is first that SPL and later the gate DAT. Traced: by cycle 40,000 the core is full and it ends as SPL-fed `DAT <-20, #0` gates.
4. *Imps.* `SPL 1` x3 + `MOV -1, 0` give 15 processes; `SPL 2 / JMP @0 / ADD #1143, -1` launch 15 imps onto a 7-point spiral in one pass; the adders die on the increment cell.

**Why it is strong.** The stone's grain keeps it off its own code without a test, its life is exactly the core over its grain, and the 7-point spiral dodges 3-point anti-imp bombs.

**Rebuild.** `seq(opening-qscan-m-88, blitz-stone-spiral-88)`. Alone the rebuilt body's process counts match the original's at every checkpoint to within a handful.

**Fidelity.** Hill 1.272 against 1.340; body 1.163 against 1.162; head-to-head 45-68-87.

**ModelWar.** 1.350 (13 points, step 1048 so the grain stays 8, 3150 passes); above Decoy Adapter against Skippy (2.81 vs 2.73).

**Lessons.** gcd(step, core) is a free timer and a free safety check at once.

## Hill summary

**Recurring patterns.**
- *The quickscan carpet decides the early race.* Seven of ten carry a decoding quickscan whose decoder writes into the bomb instruction itself, and its value is the carpet, not the scan (Talisman tests above).
- *Self-timing by self-hit.* Talisman (pass 1819), Quicksilver (pass 1868, after losing its DJN), Into the Unknown (1344), Freight Train (2437), Der Zweite Blitzkrieg (core/8 passes) and Mountain Viper (pointer meets itself) all change phase when their own pointer reaches one of their own cells. None has a counter.
- *SPL-fed bodies.* Every stone here, the vampire and the resins have an SPL 0 head and grow to thousands of processes; a hit then removes one instruction from a crowd instead of killing a loop, and a body that loses its loop line keeps working as an SPL-fed chain.
- *Fields doing two jobs.* Fan lines' B-fields as copy counters (Freight Train, Der Zweite Blitzkrieg), the increment that is the jump (Mountain Viper), decoder tables in scan lines, the burst pointer in the bomb-laying MOV, a counter that re-arms itself (Zygote).
- *Imps for ties, on 3 or 7 points.* Seven of ten launch imps; the 7-point users (Freight Train, Der Zweite Blitzkrieg) avoid the 3-point anti-imp bombs (`DAT <2667`) that four of the ten carry (Freight Train itself among them).
- *Side effects are part of the design.* Quicksilver bombs its own pump at pass 47, Elemental Dust 2's fangs hit its own pit and finally its own loop, Into the Unknown's paper clears eat its stone. The rebuilds only matched when they reproduced these, and two contracts had to allow them (`GUARD 0`, the vampire's retirement).

**What the catalog still cannot express.**
- Moore's q^2 decoder (13 pairs, tables in the launcher's SPL lines) and its stun-pit attack in the original's speed; `opening-q2-stun-88` decodes linearly and lost points here.
- Whatever makes Mountain Viper's and Zygote's Janeczek quickscans worth 0.12 and 0.25 to them; `opening-qscan-m-88` recovers only part of it there.
- Resinoid's in-place imp-growing paper tail, and Lithobolia's six-process paper with 12/6/6-cell copy windows.
- The exact timing that aligns Freight Train's two pumps with its vector spiral (found by search here, not derived).
- Why Elemental Dust 2's decoys are `DAT #pit, #1`.

**ModelWar.** None of the rebuilds comes near the live entry: Mountain Viper's one-shot 1.536, Der Zweite Blitzkrieg 1.350, Talisman 1.269, Into the Unknown 1.113, Zygote 1.091, Freight Train 0.949, against 2.16 for Decoy Adapter v6.1 and 1.67 for runner-stone-imp. All of them lose most of their points to the Lisan/orn/SEMalytics/Dave/MWClient group (0.5 to 1.3). Per opponent they do beat 2.16-level play in places: the one-shot against clu_3, Drift_v2 and NovaBomber; Into the Unknown, Zygote and Der Zweite Blitzkrieg against Skippy; Zygote against PROBE; Into the Unknown and Zygote against DriftLab. The parts that look worth trying in a ModelWar composite are the one-shot (`oneshot-88`, a scanner that does well against the Drift family and NovaBomber) and the decoding quickscan with its built-in carpet (`opening-qscan-m-88`), since a fast first strike is what lost these rebuilds their early races on the '88 hill. Neither was tried inside a ModelWar composite (not done).
