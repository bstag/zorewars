# How the nine SAL hills changed

SAL's public standings let us follow individual entrants and fields through a sequence of challenges. We sampled the opening, midpoint by challenge number, and a pinned recent endpoint on each of the nine hills. All nine had a different leader at the endpoint than at their midpoint. The midpoint's entire lineup had turned over on three hills. On Tiny LP, 11 of 15 midpoint entries were still visible, even though the leader changed. [SAL hill index](https://sal.discontinuity.info/index.php)

The intervals range from 53 to 4,928 challenges, and the hill age counters differ from the challenge IDs. These endpoints compare membership over different spans. They do not estimate turnover rates or account for any departures and returns between observations. A warrior with a different SAL ID counts as a separate entry, even when its name or author matches a previous entry. A higher score in a later field measures performance against that field. It does not establish that the later warrior would win against the earlier field.

## What the histories suggest testing

- **Beginners.** At challenge 2,168, *vamp'em* led a 23-entry field at 164.5. *Iron Gate* entered second at challenge 2,169 and *vamp'em* retained first, now at 164.4 in a 24-entry field. This is a small, inspectable transition for testing how one new opponent affects incumbent scores, and how known published designs interact with newer fields. [Before Iron Gate](https://sal.discontinuity.info/hill.php?key=94b&t=2168&how=late) · [After Iron Gate](https://sal.discontinuity.info/hill.php?key=94b&t=2169&how=late)
- **Fortress.** *Hot Needle of Inquiry* led at challenge 179 with 234.4 and at 199 with 196.4. The same leader can have a different score as the field changes. Fortress's 8,000-cell ring with 4,000 minimum separation permits only the opposite initial address, and its distinct 4,000 write limit is a separate rule. The combination warrants dedicated long-range attack and defensive-layout tests after the VM supports the write limit. [Fortress 179](https://sal.discontinuity.info/hill.php?key=94f&t=179&how=late) · [Fortress 199](https://sal.discontinuity.info/hill.php?key=94f&t=199&how=late) · [Compatibility notes](sal-hill-research.md)
- **Tourney.** Ten midpoint entries remained visible while the lead passed from *The T Machine* to *Crowning Of Atlantis*. The 8,192-cell core and 300-instruction budget make it useful for checking whether scan strides, imp spacing, boot destinations and decoy layouts still work after changing the core geometry. A step tuned against 8,000 can have a different cycle of reachable addresses under 8,192. [Tourney 101](https://sal.discontinuity.info/hill.php?key=94t&t=101&how=late) · [Tourney 203](https://sal.discontinuity.info/hill.php?key=94t&t=203&how=late)
- **Limited Process.** *D-Knight* still led at the additional challenge 509 sample, 226 IDs after the midpoint, while *Scorpion* led at 566. The eight-process cap makes process scheduling observable in any candidate boot, imp launch or replicator. Count live processes per component and measure time until the first effective attack. A full process queue can deny the next component its startup. [LP 283](https://sal.discontinuity.info/hill.php?key=lp&t=283&how=late) · [LP 509](https://sal.discontinuity.info/hill.php?key=lp&t=509&how=late) · [LP 566](https://sal.discontinuity.info/hill.php?key=lp&t=566&how=late)
- **Medium Process.** *Simplex* leads at 153 and appears third on LP at 566 with the same author ID. That is a lead for a cross-profile replay after verifying source versions. The 64-process MP cap and one P-space cell isolate process scheduling from ordinary persistent strategy memory, because the sole P-space cell holds the previous result. [MP Simplex](https://sal.discontinuity.info/warrior.php?key=mp&id=82&t=153) · [LP Simplex](https://sal.discontinuity.info/warrior.php?key=lp&id=475&t=566) · [ZoreWars P-space result](../src/engine/vm.js)
- **Metaswitch.** Endpoint challenger *X9C8* entered first with 247.6 and tied 10.5% of its rounds while the displayed field averaged 54.1% ties. The author describes it as an HSA-style scanner without a boot. It won 89 of 100 rounds against second-ranked *Replicator3*, with two ties, and lost 75 of 100 rounds against fifth-ranked *MVB9*. A static scanner gives us a useful reference for a P-space switcher. Preserve round order and P-space, then compare the switcher with each fixed strategy on the same opponent sequences. [Metaswitch 105](https://sal.discontinuity.info/hill.php?key=msw&t=105&how=late) · [X9C8's strategy and matchup results](https://sal.discontinuity.info/warrior.php?key=msw&id=105&t=105) · [P-space switching examples](https://www.corewar.info/lexicon/p-switcher.htm)
- **Nano.** The extra early sample at challenge 985 was led by *Military Grade Nano*, while the midpoint was led by *impossible* and the endpoint by *Reptiloid Conspiracy*. Participants documented a 2005 rise of small scanners and quickscanners after the earlier prevalence of clears. That provides a dated hypothesis to test with published code. Our earlier *Toy Soldier* ablation found that the same conditional redirect helped against some opponents and cost wins against others. [Nano 985](https://sal.discontinuity.info/hill.php?key=nano&t=985&how=late) · [Participants' NanoWarrior report](https://www.corewar.info/corewarrior/nw/01.htm) · [Local ablation](sal-hill-research.md#first-case-study)
- **Tiny.** None of the 25 midpoint entries were present at the endpoint, whose leader *Negative Thinking* already had an age of 216. The authors of μGP also documented how *White Noise* and the related *Larger Than Infinity* traded the lead as the opponent field changed in 2004–05. The 20-instruction budget gives us enough space to test an opener and a body as separate components, and the 800-cell core makes fast threat delivery measurable. [Tiny 1,643](https://sal.discontinuity.info/hill.php?key=tiny&t=1643&how=late) · [μGP authors' CIG 2006 paper](https://www.cse.unr.edu/~sushil/cig06/proceedings/cigv5.pdf)
- **Tiny LP.** The similar average tie percentages coexist with a change of leader. *Master of the Wins* went from first at 97 to second at 195 while *KISS* led. Tiny LP has the same core and cycle limits as Tiny but allows up to 50 instructions with just eight processes. It makes a paired experiment possible. Hold a legal 20-instruction image fixed and vary the process cap first, then use the separate Tiny LP length budget to test adaptations. [Tiny LP 97](https://sal.discontinuity.info/hill.php?key=tinylp&t=97&how=late) · [Tiny LP 195](https://sal.discontinuity.info/hill.php?key=tinylp&t=195&how=late)

## When rank changes and matchups stay fixed

On Tiny LP, *KISS* moved from sixth at challenge 97 to first at 195, and *Master of the Wins* moved from first to second. Their displayed matchup was the same on both dates. From the *KISS* perspective, it won 54.9%, lost 24.9%, and tied 20.1%. Across the two result tables, all 11 common warrior rows had the same W/L/T and both pair scores. A second check on Nano found the same result for all 47 common rows in *Reptiloid Conspiracy*'s tables at challenges 9,830 and 9,855. In these samples the field changed while the common pair scores were stable at the published precision. The matchup tables include their own set of opponents, which can differ from the “only live” hill table. [Master of the Wins at 97](https://sal.discontinuity.info/warrior.php?key=tinylp&id=3&t=97) · [At 195](https://sal.discontinuity.info/warrior.php?key=tinylp&id=3&t=195) · [Reptiloid Conspiracy at 9,830](https://sal.discontinuity.info/warrior.php?key=nano&id=9821&t=9830) · [At 9,855](https://sal.discontinuity.info/warrior.php?key=nano&id=9821&t=9855)

Keep two scores when using these histories for training. First score a candidate against a frozen reference field to isolate changes to the warrior. Then score against time-specific fields to see which eras reward its mechanisms. Add a worst-field score and the matchup spread so the average does not hide a sharp loss against a new family.

One bridge from standings to executable code is *Reptiloid Conspiracy*. SAL's Nano entry and the Koenigstuhl source agree on the name, author, and description as an evolved clear. The published program gives us a five-cell clear with several moving indirect operands to inspect and replay. The agreement corroborates identity while the archived SAL submission bytes remain unavailable from its public record. [SAL entry](https://sal.discontinuity.info/warrior.php?key=nano&id=9821&t=9855) · [Published Redcode](https://asdflkj.net/COREWAR/NANO/HILL32/reptiloid_Conspiracy.red)

## Reproducing the survey and building on it

Fetch the 27 linked pages using the pinned `key`, `t`, and `how=late` parameters. Verify that each successful page names the requested challenge or explicitly reports an empty field at that request. Read the first standings table only, keeping later killed and pushed-off tables as separate event evidence. For each row save rank, W/L/T, name, author, points, age and the numeric `id` from its warrior URL. Sort by rank, intersect the midpoint and endpoint IDs for each hill, and average the visible tie column. SAL returned HTTP 429 in the first collection pass. Cached responses and a slower sequential follow-up completed the sample.

To measure rates, sample matched challenge windows around verified entry and exit events, with calendar timestamps where available. For matchup changes, request an individual warrior's result table at each timestamp and use fixed-opponent rows alongside the full field. To study how a particular mechanism changes outcomes, link verifiable published code, preserve a frozen field and its rules, and run the original next to a matched ablation. Track strategy descriptions and source availability separately so a metadata-only entry still counts in the historical standings.
