# Koenigstuhl 94x hill, top 30: source-level breakdown

Core 55440 = 2^4·3^2·5·7·11, 10000 processes, 500000 cycles, length 200, P-space allowed on the hill.
Target tournament: same core, **50 instructions, no P-space**.

How this was checked: all 30 sources read in full; every file assembled with `zorewars compile --rules 94x-hill` (lengths below are assembled lengths); behaviour of each component confirmed by running it alone in the ZoreWars VM from the component's entry point (process counts, boot destinations, self-conversion times). "measured" = seen in such a solo run; "read" = from the source only. Things I could not confirm are marked **uncertain**.

Two general facts that correct common assumptions:

- **No 3-point or 7-point imps exist on this core.** An N-point ring needs gcd(N, 55440) = 1, so the smallest ring is **13-point**. Steps actually used (all verified, N·step ≡ 1 mod 55440): 13-pt 34117, 17-pt 35873, 19-pt 29179, 31-pt 32191. (18481 and 7921 are not imp steps here.) 27721 = core/2 + 1 is the 2-point "ring" step and is what most anti-imp DATs decrement by.
- **First-round choice of the P-switchers** (what would run every round if P-space cell 0 always read -1 and the state cell read 0): 7 of 9 pick a scanner. See the P-space column.

## Per-warrior notes

### 1. Fatamorgana X (Zul Nadzri) - 200, P-space, P-switcher over four warriors

Entry is "SureWin": on round 1 (`ldp #0` = -1) it stores a marker in P-cell 300 and scans backward one cell a cycle for the first non-empty cell; if that cell is not its own `count` line it steps off its last instruction into empty core and dies. Round 2 (lost, marker still set) clears the marker and executes a DAT. Measured against Tsunami: lost round 1 at cycle 494, round 2 at cycle 8, normal play from round 3. This is a self-recognition handshake (one copy idles in `jmp $0`, the other dies), so **with no P-space it never fights**; the exact match condition in a self-fight is **uncertain**. From round 3 "Predator" keeps a score box per warrior (win +1, loss -1; 4 ties in a row count as a loss), then runs the warrior with the highest box (`slt.a` chain), with a brainwash sentinel (651 in cell 15) that reinitialises everything. The four warriors: (a) Fire and Ice's airbag stone (see #2), identical code; (b) Recon 2: copied 27720 away (15 cells, 1 process), a cmp scanner testing two pairs per 5-instruction loop (.8c), step 4876 (gcd 4), pair gap 6, SPL wipe on a hit, DAT clear after a 25000-loop timer (125k cycles; measured); (c) "ccpaper": 9 processes (`spl 1` + three `mov -1, 0`), a launcher copies the 9-line body 9108 away, three silk stages (9108, 4730, 777), then `mov 2, <-1` drops `dat {34117, <34117`; (d) The Truth Is Out There II (see #2) booted 19255 away. After booting, the scanner arms shuffle or blank a handful of cells in the abandoned image (`mov X, X+6`, `mov gula, ...`); purpose **uncertain** (gap-6 camouflage against cmp scanners is my guess). Nearest blocks: `switch` (not `switch-table`), `pair-wipe-scanner` (Recon 2), `silk-cc`/`silk3` (paper), `hill-stone` + `finisher-dclear` (stone).

```redcode
WL      seq #1, start        ; win?
lose    sub.a #2, 1
win     add.a #1, 1
incrloc stp #0, #0           ; score box of the warrior just played
...
compare slt.a compb, compa   ; pick the best of four boxes
        jmp bcd, }exec
x1      stp #pnum, #pbox     ; round 1: handshake
seek    jmz.f seek, {count
count   sne.i #pmula, {count
        jmp $0, {count
```

### 2. Fire and Ice II (David Moore) - 200, P-space, scanner / stone

P^3 brain: `ldp` result and state, `mod.ba *pTab2, pTable` reduces the stored number by 6 (after a loss), 7 (win) or 5 (tie) to pick a row; the row's B-field is stored as the next number and `jmp }0` jumps through the row's A-field. Rows 0-2 = scanner, 3-5 = stone (row 6 unreachable). Transitions (W/L/T): 0: 1/5/0, 1: 2/0/1, 2: 2/1/2, 3: 3/4/4, 4: 3/5/4, 5: 3/0/0. So the scanner is dropped after two losses, the stone after three. First round: `mod 1` gives row 0, which falls through to the scanner; that is what a no-P-space run would always play. **Scanner, The Truth Is Out There II** (20 lines + 9 boot, 1 process, booted 19241 away, boot erases its pointers with `mod.f #a, #b`): a .5c F-scan (`add.a #13378` + `jmz.f -1, }sPtr`, net step 13379, gcd 1). On a find it walks backward a cell a cycle to the start of the body (`jmn.f #0, {sTop`), lays an SPL carpet forward until the first empty cell, drops an `stp.ab >0, #0` brainwash bomb, then adds the found cell's B-field to the pointer and repeats there, which chases an imp ring arm by arm. After one full pass (110,880 cycles) it finds itself (pointer 0) and falls into `spl #0` + d-clear. **Airbag stone** (26 lines with boot, measured): a two-part boot puts the 5-line loop 43990 away with 5 processes, the bomb `mov dStep, 1` 11 cells after it, and a 3-line d-clear at loop + dStep. Each pass writes two bombs dStep apart (the second through the first bomb's A-field), stepping 2·dStep; dStep = 53421 = -2019, gcd 3, so 18480 targets at .5c, done in 36,960 cycles. `jmn.b dTop, dTop+dbPos` watches the remote bomb cell: when it reads zero (overwritten by an enemy DAT, or by the stone's own final pass) the loop falls into `jmp dPtr+dStep`, the 5-process d-clear with `dat <27721`. The boot's three `>`/`{` operands tick three cells on the stone's own lattice; purpose **uncertain**. No decoys; 127 blank cells separate the three parts. Nearest blocks: `switch-table` (PACKED 1 is exactly this encoding), `hill-stone` (REMOTE bomb) + `finisher-dclear`, recipe `stone-then-clear`; our `airbag-stone` block is a different mechanism. Scanner: none (nearest `scanner-pendulum` for wipe-until-empty).

```redcode
dTop   add.ab #dStep*2, dPtr
       mov      >2,    @dPtr      ; bomb at X
dPtr   mov      <1,    *(dStep*2) ; bomb at X + dStep, via the first bomb's A
       jmn.b  dTop,     dTop+dbPos ; airbag: remote bomb still there?
dLast  jmp dPtr+dStep, <dGate-1   ; no: into the d-clear
dBomb  mov dStep, 1
```

### 3. Fire and Brimstone (Moore / Gunnell) - 193, P-space, Kenshin / stone

Same brain and same stone as #2 (state cell 123 instead of 2001); the scanner arm is Kenshin, so the first-round / no-P-space choice is Kenshin. Kenshin's boot splits to the destination first (`spl LEAP`, 32003 away) and copies behind the running child with ten `mov.i >sBoot, }sBoot`, then erases itself. Kenshin is one process in a 3-instruction loop that does two jobs: `mov *tail, }bptr` writes one bomb at a running clear pointer, `add.a #STEP, @hptr` / `jmz.f trash, *head` tests one cell (STEP 15331, gcd 1, .33c). A find copies the scan pointer into the clear pointer (`mov.a head, bptr`, skipped by the `slt` when the find is its own 14 cells), so the carpet always runs and is re-aimed by each find. Each time the scan pointer comes back to 0 (166,320 cycles) `jmp @hptr, }tail` bumps tail's A-field, so the bomb goes `spl #0,}0`, then `jmp #0,}0`, then `dat 0,0`: three passes fit in 500,000 cycles. Nearest blocks: `blur-scanner` (carpet one cell per lap while scanning) + `boot` EARLY 1 (copy-ahead); the bomb ladder is none.

```redcode
head  slt    #START, #tail-bptr+5   ; A-field is the scan pointer
      mov.a  head,   bptr           ; aim the clear at the find
trash mov    *tail,  }bptr          ; one carpet cell per lap
      add.a  #STEP,  @hptr
      jmz.f  trash,  *head
hptr  jmn.a  @hptr,  head
      jmp    @hptr,  }tail          ; lap done: next bomb type
tail  spl    #0,     }0
```

### 6. Azathoth (John Metcalf) - 22, no P-space, paper/stone

No opening. An 11-line fan boot: `spl 2 / spl 1 / spl 1` makes 6 processes that copy 6 cells of paper to +6172 and 6 cells of stone to -5770 one cell each; five SPLs through the copy pointer send 30 processes to the stone, the last line (`djn.f pboot`) sends the 6 to the paper. Stone (4 lines + `spl #0` + bomb): `mov bomb, @ptr` drops `dat <sstep, >1` every 2·23431 (gcd 2, .25c); `mov {x, }first` writes through the A-field of the next target cell (in empty core a no-op, on enemy code it hits whatever that instruction points at); `djn.f` adds a decrement stream. After 10230 passes (about 41k cycles of its own time, measured) the bomb lands on `hit` and the stone becomes `spl #0 / mov bomb, @ptr / dat`, a forward clear that spends one process per cell. Paper (5 lines, 6 processes): each lap copies itself 16342 away (A-postincrement pointers), copies the 12 cells after itself behind the copy, and loops, the distance growing 18 a lap; a copy whose cell at paper+6 is not zero falls off its end instead of looping. The stone shares the queue with the paper, so it only speeds up when the paper is stunned ("sleeping chaos"). Why the paper copies 12 extra cells: **uncertain**. Nearest blocks: `fanboot-paper-stone` (the recipe `paper-stone`; Arvon credits this warrior), `timed-stone` END 1.

```redcode
        spl    #0,         6
stone   mov    bomb,       @ptr
hit     sub.x  #sstep*2,   @stone
ptr     mov    {-23878,    }first
        djn.f  @hit,       }ptr
bomb    dat    <sstep,     >1
paper   spl    pstep,      {x
        mov    }x,         }paper
```

### 8. Denial (David Moore) - 196, no P-space, stone/imp (24 live lines)

Boot (13 lines): places the bomb 5 cells past the stone's end and the two imp instructions, fans to 6 then 24 processes, copies 6 cells of stone 30133 away and 6 cells of launcher 30361 away; 48 processes enter the stone, 24 the launcher (measured). Stone: `spl #5` on top of a loop that writes `dat hStep, >1` at X and, through that bomb's A-field, at X + 859, stepping 1718 (859 is prime, so every cell is on the lattice; .4c), with a DJN stream; the loop re-enters its own SPL, so it keeps taking process slots. After 11650 passes the bomb lands on `hHit` and it becomes the one-cell-per-process clear. Launcher: one loop feeds two spirals at once: `add.f` steps the A-field by 29179 (19-point, B-imps `mov.i #5, i19`) and the B-field by 34117 (13-point, A-imps `mov.i #i13, *0`); `spl` launches one, `jmp @-1` the other. 170 blank cells separate the three parts. Nearest blocks: `fanboot-stone-imp`, `dual-launcher` (ours is one-pass, this one is continuous), `timed-stone`.

### 10. Discord (John Metcalf) - 16, no P-space, scanner

## Patterns

- **The top five are all P-switchers, and nine of the top 30.** Seven use the same 5-line P^3 dispatcher with a table of 7 to 15 rows. Take P-space away and each collapses to its first-round arm: a scanner in seven cases (Truth II, Kenshin, Arrow X, 55HSB, Katafutr scanner, Stalker, Tsunami), paper/imp for Katafutr, and nothing at all for Fatamorgana, which throws its first two rounds on a handshake.
- **The arms of the switchers are small.** Airbag stone with boot 26 lines, Kenshin 23, Arrow X 11 (24 booted), Truth II 29, Denial 25, Tsunami 9, Stalker 20. Any one, and most pairs, fit in 50 instructions; what does not fit is the selector plus three or more arms.
- **Scanners dominate (19 of 30 carry one) and almost all are one process.** Two styles lead: carpet-while-scanning (blur family, 8) and F-scan with wipe-until-empty (6). Only three are one-shots. Quickscans are rare (3 of 30) and absent from the top 11: with 55440 cells a 24 to 33 cell probe seldom pays for its 20 to 45 lines.
- **Imps mean 13 points or more.** Seven warriors use 34117; pumps that launch one imp per pass (9) outnumber one-pass binary launches (3). Rings this long are slow to close, so imp users pair them with a multi-process stone, and scanners carry explicit imp answers: following B-fields (Truth II, Katafutr), a clear stepping by the imp step (55HSB), or `dat <27721` / `dat <34117` in the final clear.
- **Stones are multi-process, time-limited, and end as a clear.** Moore's stone runs 36,960 cycles and jumps to a d-clear; Metcalf's 4-line stone bombs itself after a set count and becomes `spl #0 / mov / dat`; the Olivia-family stones fall through into `mov bomb, >ptr / djn.f`. Bomb steps are chosen with a small gcd (2 to 18) so the lattice misses the stone's own few cells.
- **Everything bigger than a 16-line scanner boots**, usually 2000 to 44000 cells away, with copy pointers that double as jump vectors and are erased afterwards (`mod.f`, `div.f`, `sub.f x, x`). Copy-ahead boots (split first, copy behind the child) appear in Kenshin and Arrow X.
- **The 200-cell image is used for distance, not code.** Fourteen warriors are mostly blank padding that puts boot, brain and payload 50 to 190 cells apart; five fill it with 63 to 168 decoy SPLs. A 50-instruction limit removes both: no spread image, no large decoy field. Run-time decoy makers (3 to 11 lines) are the affordable substitute.
- **What a 50-line, no-P-space entry gives up:** the selector (so one strategy, or two run side by side, must cover what the table covered), brainwash bombs (STP appears in six warriors' bombs), the big static decoys and the padding, and realistically the quickscan. What it keeps: every engine here, since the complete non-P-space warriors at ranks 6, 7, 10, 15, 22 and 23 are 11 to 25 lines.
- **Fixed two-part mixes already rank high without P-space:** paper/stone (Azathoth 6th, Despair X 23rd) and stone/imp (Void Runner 7th, Denial 8th) are the best-placed non-switchers, both built on a lockstep fan boot that fits in 11 to 13 lines.
