## The two Labarga entries have the same published opponent results

*Unknown* and *The two* arrived about nine minutes apart. At challenge 195, their matchup tables report identical W/L/T and both scores against all 14 other displayed entrants, including the challenger *Feather*. Their match with each other has asymmetric results, with 46.8% wins, 42.5% losses and 10.7% ties from *Unknown*'s perspective. This is a strong lead for grouping them together when splitting historical data into training and holdout sets. Their exact source relationship cannot be established from the results. ## Timing the five published entries

These are solo VM turns with each image loaded at address zero under the 800-cell, eight-process, 8,000-cycle Tiny LP profile. The first external `MOV.I` is the first whole-instruction copy outside the loaded image. It can be a boot, replication or attack write. The attack column follows the source's named clear, bomb or scan-attack path. An attack can hit the original image in a quiet core. All addresses are absolute for this load at zero. [Round runner](../src/engine/battle.js) · [VM write provenance](../src/engine/vm.js)

| Published warrior | Loaded cells | First external whole-cell copy | First remote execution | First attack write | Reached eight processes | Solo result |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| KISS | 50 | Turn 2 to 387 | Turn 13 at 383 | Turn 15, remote SPL to 445 | 24 | Survived 8,000 |
| Master of the Wins | 43 | Turn 7 to 291 | Turn 12 at 289 | Turn 12, remote stone DAT to 453 | 166 | Last process died at turn 4,441 on a DAT written by its own stone |
| FragPaper 800 | 50 | Turn 1 to 558 | Turn 11 at 556 | Turn 13, home paper DAT to 372 | 137 | Survived 8,000 |
| tiny Roskilde | 39 | Turn 3 to 371 | Turn 21 at 526 | Turn 21, remote stone DAT to 435 | 217 | Survived 8,000 |
| ugly redcode | 50 | Turn 1,720 to 654 | None | Turn 45, scanner JMP to local cell 21 | Never | Survived 8,000 with one process |

The first remote execution and the first attack capture different roles. *FragPaper 800* boots a stone on turn 1, but its home paper makes the first attack. *tiny Roskilde* copies part of its paper first and then attacks from a remote stone on turn 21. *KISS* has both clears running before its process queue is full. A Tiny LP block that promises these behaviors should report which deployed process did the work and when, alongside its total process count. [FragPaper 800 source](https://www.corewar.info/tournament/round22/fpaper.red) · [tiny Roskilde source](https://www.corewar.info/tournament/round22/roskilde.red) · [KISS deployment analysis](hill-block-review.md#sal-leaders-with-separately-published-sources)

I also planted a single `JMP.B #0,#19` marker after loading the solo warrior and before executing its first instruction. In *ugly redcode* the marker at 103 triggers the quickscan, which replaces it with a JMP bomb on turn 5. In *Master of the Wins* the marker at 600 triggers its multiply-decoded quickscan. Its first DAT lands at 601 on turn 11, then its first remote stone runs on turn 19. An empty-core run and a planted-hit run therefore measure different timings, and the probe address is part of the record.
