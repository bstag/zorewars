# What SAL history can teach ZoreWars

SAL supplies a dated record of who challenged, what the field looked like, and how warriors scored. Published archives supply runnable warriors. Joining those records where the identity is verifiable gives us both the historical result and a way to test explanations for it. [SAL](https://sal.discontinuity.info/index.php) · [Koenigstuhl](https://asdflkj.net/COREWAR/koenigstuhl.html)

## What we ran

The CLI accepts custom core size, cycle limit, process cap, program length, separation and P-space size. The numeric settings in eight rows above fit these options. Fortress's write limit needs VM support. Shared P-space via `PIN` is also unsupported, and the SAL match schedule and historical placement sequence were outside this check. The built-in `lp` profile uses a 100-instruction tournament entry limit and disables P-space, so it differs from SAL LP's advertised settings. The local-folder hill loader also relaxes opponent length and P-space restrictions. Strictly validate a corpus with its own profile before evaluating a local field. [CLI settings](../cli/zorewars.js) · [Named profiles](../src/engine/constants.js) · [Hill opponent settings](../cli/devtools-context.js) · [pMARS compliance](../COMPLIANCE.md)

Using SAL Nano and Tiny settings with strict assertions, every Redcode file in the corresponding Koenigstuhl tarballs assembled. The counts were 499 Nano and 436 Tiny, after excluding 34 Nano AppleDouble metadata files. Real archive pairs also ran through the normal `battle` command for 250 rounds at seed 124. Nano's *Reptiloid Conspiracy* scored 147 wins, 96 losses and seven ties against *Forbes*. Tiny's *Little Fluffy Clouds* scored 92 wins, 49 losses and 109 ties against *Imp .X-ing*. These are local smoke tests. Historical standings and simulator parity require their own checks. [Nano sources](https://asdflkj.net/COREWAR/NANO/nano.tar.gz) · [Tiny sources](https://asdflkj.net/COREWAR/TINY/tiny.tar.gz)

## First case study

The authors of NanoWarrior documented a clear-heavy opening era and a wave of Nano quickscanners and scanners in 2005. John Metcalf's *Type-1* scanned backward with step −5. The report credits it with strong performance against conventional decrementing clears and some early scanners, and describes declining rank as quickscanners spread. It prints the code. Koenigstuhl also lists *Type-1*, *Toy Soldier* and *Bombus Sylvestris* with source links. This gives us an explicit historical explanation to test using matchup records and a controlled replay. [NanoWarrior #1](https://www.corewar.info/corewarrior/nw/01.htm) · [Koenigstuhl Nano](https://asdflkj.net/COREWAR/NANO/hill32_rec.html)

For the replay, use verified published clears, reverse scanners and quickscanners from the relevant period. Compare *Type-1* against each family and vary scan direction or step while holding everything else fixed. Track the cycles until the first enemy hit, where the first bomb lands, and whether the initial strike converts into a kill. Then compare that measured matchup pattern with SAL's actual W/L/T when the source identity is known. In Nano, relative start offsets 5 through 75 and both first-mover orders give 142 placement/order combinations for stateless warriors. A P-space sequence needs a fixed round order and explicit reset policy. [Metcalf's Nano benchmark notes](https://corewar.co.uk/nano.htm) · [Koenigstuhl P-space notes](https://asdflkj.net/COREWAR/koenigstuhl.html)

As a first ablation, we ran *Toy Soldier* at all 142 placement/order combinations against four published opponents with fresh state for each round. Its opening conditionally executes a `MOV` that redirects the clear pointer. We held its image length fixed and replaced the opening `SEQ` with a `NOP` to always redirect or a `JMP` to always skip the redirect. Taking the redirect adds the `MOV` turn, just as it does in the original. [Published Nano field](https://asdflkj.net/COREWAR/NANO/hill32_rec.html) · [ZoreWars round runner](../src/engine/battle.js)

| Opponent | Original W/L/T | Always redirect | Never redirect |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Type-1 | 64/73/5 | 69/67/6 | 60/76/6 |
| Bombus Sylvestris | 55/83/4 | 44/94/4 | 40/98/4 |
| Reptiloid Conspiracy | 69/64/9 | 29/96/17 | 58/77/7 |
| Forbes | 48/81/13 | 36/91/15 | 50/81/11 |

The conditional opener beat both fixed branches against *Bombus Sylvestris* and *Reptiloid Conspiracy*. A fixed branch won more rounds against *Type-1* and *Forbes*. These four matchups show when the pointer choice can pay for an instruction and where it costs wins. A wider field is needed to judge the design overall.

## Catalog and experiments

1. Start with pinned Nano snapshots before, during and after the documented transition, plus a recent snapshot and a Tiny LP comparison. Keep `(hill, challenge, view)` for snapshots and `(hill, warrior ID)` for entries. Record timestamps, original URLs, fetched content hashes, rule settings, ranks, W/L/T, score, age, and entry and exit events. The current rules table can appear on an old snapshot, so verify historical rule changes separately.
2. Match public code by name, author and provenance, retain the source URL and compiled fingerprint, and distinguish exact matches, probable matches and metadata-only entries. Keep self-described strategy, source-derived mechanism and measured behavior in separate fields. Report the fraction of each snapshot with verified code and retain missing entries in the historical data.
3. Study turnover, ages at eviction, which incumbent pairs changed rank, strategy mix, and pairwise matchup shifts around a dated arrival. Compare the same incumbent against a fixed reference panel so changes in the opponent mix are visible. Separate an author's declared lineage from similarity inferred from code. Adjacent snapshots share many warriors, so split validation by time and keep clones or near relatives together.
4. Freeze source-backed opponent panels by era. Use early eras to propose blocks, later eras and source-held-out opponents to test them. Measure overall score, the worst era and family, win/tie mix, and what wins disappear when a mechanism is removed. Report these as controlled battles on the published subset. SAL standings and match tables remain the record of historical performance.

The existing archive cache already writes source receipts and SHA-256 hashes. The strategy trends and hill recipe book show how ZoreWars combines code tags with a mechanism reading. The current tagger embeds 8,000-cell assumptions and requires six comparisons for a quickscan, so it misses a one-comparison Nano quickscan such as *Toy Soldier*. Give the tagger the actual profile and check it against a small hand-labeled Nano set before measuring a trend. ## Implications for blocks

Treat core size, program length, process cap and cycle limit as inputs to a block's contract. Normalize offsets for the actual ring, check the stride's reachable cells and self-hits after layout, and measure the first useful write and the process budget. A five-instruction warrior makes a separate launcher or handoff expensive. *Bombus Sylvestris* combines a scan, one bomb and a clear in five cells, while *Type-1* uses a reverse scan and wipe. These make plausible Nano leaves with shared instruction fields and behavior assertions rather than requiring a new linking model. Tiny's 20 instructions give room to test short openings and finishers. Tiny LP and SAL LP expose starvation in a fan, boot or paper with eight process slots. A verified short switch belongs with the P-space profile it needs. [Published Nano designs](https://www.corewar.info/corewarrior/nw/01.htm) · [Block contracts](block-algebra.md)

We screened the 115 registered leaves individually at default parameters by checking their tree contracts, linking, checking the length, assembling and running the full gate with custom settings. Nano had four assemble within the budget, with two gate passes, `imp` and `imp-gate`. The compact imp pump failed its retirement check. The stone failed a 100-bombed-cell threshold that an 80-cell core cannot meet. Some checkpoints are also at cycle 1,000, beyond Nano's entire round. Tiny had 39 leaves assemble within budget and 33 gate passes, including clears, paper, scanner, imp ring and silk-imps. The audit used the lower-level linker and gate because the ordinary build and recipe paths require a named rule profile. These are coverage counts for individual defaults and say nothing about tuned assemblies or hill strength. [Block algebra](block-algebra.md) · [Gate](../tools/algebra/gate.js)

Tiny is a first target for regular recipe and evolution support because it already has useful leaf coverage. Nano calls for dedicated single-image leaves and profile-aware behavior checks. A Nano clear might gate the first useful write, wrap coverage and survival through its first pass. Its requirements can be expressed within the actual 800-cycle round.

For each proposed leaf, record its exact source or clean-room derivation, compatible profiles, first attack deadline, required processes, mutable fields, and protected cells. Gate the intended behavior on each supported profile, then compare it with a matched ablation against frozen eras. For example, test forward versus reverse scan, a stun followed by clear versus immediate DAT clear, and a paper with and without a colocated bombing write. A genuine portability gain survives both the compact core and the process-limited field. A profile-specific improvement can still justify a profile-specific leaf.
