# Classic Core War evolvers and ZoreWars

The clearest gaps are **resuming a full run**, **inspecting a live population**, and **letting separate breeding populations develop and exchange warriors**. ZoreWars already has raw-instruction and typed-tree evolution, a behavior archive, Red Queen and league opponents, and repeated evaluation before promoting candidates. The historical tools provide concrete designs for the parts our engine and CLI do not expose yet. [ZoreWars evolution architecture](../tools/evolution/README.md) · [Training and reproducibility](warrior-training.md)

## What each evolver adds to the comparison

| Evolver | Documented behavior | ZoreWars comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **YACE — Martin Ankerl** | Ankerl published Tiny results from a random population and later announced hill-based selection. In a firsthand test of 0.02, Terry Newton observed configurable periodic population exports and automatic extraction of the best warrior on a separate test set. Later versions also generated local hill reports. The surviving FreeBSD port description credits Exhaust. [Ankerl's random-population results](https://gallium.inria.fr/~doligez/corewar/post/Evolver4870x200.txt) · [Ankerl's hill-selection announcement](https://gallium.inria.fr/~doligez/corewar/post/Evolver520x200.txt) · [Newton's YACE runs](https://newton.freehostia.com/net/corewar/evol/#yace) · [FreeBSD port](https://www.freshports.org/games/yace/) | We score against fixed fields and validate finalists on fresh placements. Add regular population snapshots and optional independent evaluations of intermediate champions so a strong candidate that is later displaced still survives the run. The YACE reports establish population export. Exact restoration of its random-number state remains unverified. |
| **CCAI — Barkley Vowk** | Pracner and colleagues describe the CCAI corpus they analyzed as an island-model run that used Optimax. Christian Schmidt's community catalog credits CCAI with the first evolved entries to make the '94nop hill. The public abstract of the 2004 Vowk, Wait and Schmidt paper establishes a framework for human-competitive warriors. It leaves implementation details and their mapping to named tools open. [CCAI corpus study, §4](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221506252_Categorizing_Evolved_CoreWar_Warriors_Using_EM_and_Attribute_Evaluation) · [Schmidt's evolver catalog](https://www.corewar.info/evolve.htm) · [Vowk, Wait and Schmidt](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/238669885_An_Evolutionary_Approach_Generates_Human_Competitive_Corewar_Programs) | The league evolves groups with different objectives. A CCAI-style experiment would give several populations the same objective, select parents within each, and periodically exchange a few warriors. ZoreWars already accepts fixed training fields. |
| **JNSL — attributed to Barkley Vowk** | A separate manual, source release, or result explicitly labeled JNSL could not be verified. The ICPC records label Vowk's entries there as *ewon*. [ICPC submission labels](https://www.corewar.info/tournament/icpc1/) | Feature parity is undetermined. |
| **REBS — Terry Newton** | The pMARS-backed Bash evolver documents local competition, circle and grid populations, color maps, and parent, origin and generation tags. Its optional species color changes when the base opcode sequence or entry point changes. Newton published actual Nano output and its results. [Newton's REBS documentation](https://newton.freehostia.com/net/corewar/evol/rebs.html) | We have lineage IDs and measured behavior bins. The useful addition is a live view of which families are expanding or disappearing and what changed in a selected child. REBS's species tags and our behavior bins would offer different views of the same population. |
| **Fizzle — Terry Newton** | Its fuzzy ring varies from global opponent selection to immediate neighbors. Newton exposed instruction frequencies and multi-point mutation, and offered peer matches, evaluation against one sampled test warrior, or evaluation against the full fixed test set. [Newton's Fizzle notes](https://newton.freehostia.com/net/corewar/evol/#fizzle) | We have scratch and fixed-field objectives, operator selection and repeated screening. Add local opponent sampling as an explicit population option and expose the per-ruleset instruction-generation probabilities to experiments. |
| **RedMaker — Terry Newton** | RedMaker 2 displays a live population map and lets the operator pick, list, benchmark and run a warrior in pMARS visualization. It can require every admitted warrior to beat a named opponent. Newton reported that its default `JMP 0` target favored trivial clears. [Newton's RedMaker notes](https://newton.freehostia.com/net/corewar/evol/#redmaker) | Our replay and match diagnosis can serve the same inspection workflow once evolution candidates link into them. Add an optional admission condition over a small panel of rivals so it measures several behaviors. |
| **RedRace — Dave Hillis** | The ICPC organizers record *Mendacious Dahl* and *Mimetic Midwife* as RedRace outputs, finishing 8th and 24th in the same 8,000-cell, 20-instruction contest. The Inria archive preserves Hillis's *h1_36* attribution. A contemporary paper by Corno and colleagues describes RedRace testing candidates against a target hill, retaining and restoring successful warriors through Valhalla and Resurrection, and managing multiple populations. The latter details are a secondary account of RedRace. [ICPC final standings and rules](https://www.corewar.info/tournament/icpc1/) · [Inria warrior archive](https://gallium.inria.fr/~doligez/corewar/by-name/complete.htm) · [Corno et al., 2005](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=6026078bb5915689d4a4efcc26d541617f53182a&repid=rep1&type=pdf) | Our archives retain elites, and our champions can become future opponents. A distinct experiment would reintroduce earlier candidates as parents when a population stalls or loses diversity, then compare against current parent selection. |
| **CRE — Loren Jamison** | Jamison's announcement says CRE 1.0 R2 offered three selectable evolution methods. The 1.2 Beta Release 2 notes document a configurable mutation count per line and the switch to Ankerl's optimized Exhaust. [Jamison's announcement](https://forums.tomshardware.com/threads/my-corewar-evolver-cre.169565/) · [CRE release notes](https://sourceforge.net/p/cre/news/) · [CRE 1.x source](https://github.com/sornerol/Corewars-Redcode-Evolver-1.x) | We already have multiple search modes and backend selection. Its clearest relevant difference is direct control over mutations per line. Our instruction-level edits and tree mutations give us a starting point for that experiment. |

## Work to prioritize

1. **Checkpoint whole instruction populations at generation boundaries.** Save a versioned record of the RNG state, generation, population and archive, lineage, operator credits, champions and league state, plus a manifest of the rules, opponents and score settings. A stopped and resumed seeded run should reproduce the same next births, elites and finalists as an uninterrupted run. Tree ladders already restart at rungs from saved seed trees. [Evolution architecture](../tools/evolution/README.md) · [Training reproducibility](warrior-training.md)
2. **Put an Evolution view next to the native arena.** Read the checkpoint and progress records so a user can see archive coverage and champion changes, select a parent and child, inspect the Redcode diff, and open a representative matchup in replay. Use ZoreWars genome IDs and existing matchup diagnosis for the first version. [Evolution evaluation and lineage](../tools/evolution/instructions/evaluation.js) · [Newton's interactive RedMaker](https://newton.freehostia.com/net/corewar/evol/#redmaker)
3. **Run a migration experiment.** Compare the current global parent pool with independent islands and infrequent migration, using the same total evaluation budget. Measure archive occupancy, lineage concentration and held-out performance. Add Fizzle's local ring as another treatment if islands produce a measurable change. Here the ring refers to which warriors are neighbors in a breeding population. Our simulated torus changes how Redcode addresses memory during a match. [Current population selection](../tools/evolution/instructions/population.js) · [Fizzle's localness](https://newton.freehostia.com/net/corewar/evol/#fizzle)
4. **Test configurable mutation and admission.** Expose an optional ruleset-aware opcode, mode and modifier distribution and compare it with the current defaults. For staged admission, retain the existing compilation and survival checks, then test a cheap screen against a rotating small panel before submitting promising survivors to the full training field. Use the held-out set to see whether those choices generalize. Existing racing already spends more rounds on close promotions, and tree evolution already has a behavior gate. [Instruction operators](../tools/genes.js) · [Scratch seed survival](../tools/evolution/instructions/seeds.js) · [Tree search](../tools/evolution/trees/search.js)

## Benchmark protocol

The historical results establish that these designs produced competitive warriors in specific settings. We have yet to run the requested tools side by side. Freeze a published Nano, Tiny and '94nop panel with its rules and a separate held-out field for each. Archive the source, starting populations where supported, placements, seed list, versions, and exact commands. Where an older tool controls its own placements, record its protocol and reevaluate its emitted champions with the common holdout runner. First measure rounds per second on identical match inputs to compare simulators such as pMARS, Exhaust and ZoreWars. Then measure held-out score at equal evaluation budgets and time to a predeclared held-out score across multiple independent runs to compare the complete evolvers. Keep the seed budget, elapsed time and training-match count in the report. [Existing comparison runner](../scripts/compare-evolution.js) · [Prior measured experiments](evolution-experiments.md) · [SAL rules and source coverage](sal-hill-research.md)

Start with Tiny and a simple REBS-style local replacement baseline on the same ZoreWars battle backend. That gives a direct test of how much the current selection and archive improve search at the same cost. In our previous ICWS'88 experiment, tournament GA led on fresh placements against the training panel, and species MAP-Elites led on the held-out panel. The small experiment used three evolution seeds and matched offspring schedules. A Tiny comparison should match battle-round budgets as well. [ICWS'88 experiment](evolution-experiments.md)
