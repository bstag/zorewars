How many warriors exist?

Thousands. Core War has been played since 1984, and players have published their programs to newsgroups, tournament archives and "hills" (standing tournaments) ever since. The two big public archives:

5,444entries on the Koenigstuhl "infinite hills", where nothing is ever pushed off. Some warriors appear on more than one hill.
Koenigstuhl
1,106of them on the ICWS'94 "no P-space" hill, the closest match to ZoreWars' rules.
94nop hill
1,689warriors in Planar's (Damien Doligez's) archive, collected from the 1980s and 1990s.
Planar's archive
85issues of the Core Warrior newsletter (1995–2003), full of strategy write-ups.
Core Warrior

Almost all of them belong to a handful of strategy families. This page covers those families with one working example each.

About the code on this page. The archives don't say whether their warriors may be redistributed. So every warrior here is our own implementation of a classic idea, written for the ZoreWars VM and tested in it, and each card links to the original that inspired it. You can still battle the originals: Fight in the Hall of Fame (or Import… in the arena, or zorewars battle classic:thermite) fetches a warrior from its archive when you use it, and it stays in your browser. 34 of the 35 originals below load and run unchanged, including FOR loops and P-space. The exception is Frontwards, which was written for the larger '94x hill rules.

Counts checked on 24 October 2026. Koenigstuhl was last updated 23 July 2026.

The most recent name is worth noticing. Arvon, credited to "Stefan Kammann & Forge-AI", topped the Koenigstuhl 94nop recursive ranking in 2026. Its strategy notes describe a quickscan whose parameters came from a data-driven coverage model, followed by a silk paper and a stone. AI-assisted warriors are already competing at the top: exactly the kind of loop the ZoreWars CLI is built for.
