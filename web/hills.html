Read the interfaces between blocks

  • '88 and 8,192-cell ICWS. Talisman and Eye of the Storm T use an ADD-based quickscan decoder for 36 probes. Our opening-qscan-add-88 checks both sides of each pair and passes the decoded address to a following block. The 8,192-cell port of the original also changes the three-point imp step from 2,667 to 2,731.
  • ICWS/64. Twimp launches two three-point imp formations with the same step and different phases. binary-twin-imp verifies eight ordered entrants from two seeds with no extra processes needed for arithmetic.
  • 94nop. In Arvon and pst v4, inspect the quickscan, the process fan that copies the payloads, and the stone and paper that run after boot. Try changing the opening while preserving the copy order and the starting state of the two bodies.
  • Limited Process. R.O.U.S. gets copy scheduling and a remote scatter write into a small paper. lp-paper is an analogue. To measure the scatter write, replace it with a NOP that still advances both operand pointers. This holds the rest of the copy schedule steady.
  • P-space and X. Sunset and Fire and Ice II use tables that select attacks from the previous result and a stored state. switch-table lets an author specify the payload and next state independently for each row. CBD also needs shared PIN semantics, which the engine does not implement.
  • Tiny and Nano. Tiny still has space for short stages. Every cell spent on a probe on Nano leaves one less for the attack. In Toy Soldier, a single comparison decides whether to redirect the clear pointer. In the workshop below, the scanner and bomber share a pointer field.
  • Odd. Happy New Year combines a decoy, an SPL blur, and a DAT clear. Use the source to study the handoff between them. For replay, check the original tournament's rules because Odd collects programs built for different events.

Packed controller and replay results

Tiny LP's KISS sends one worker home and one to the remote copy after the boot. Against Master of the Wins, this split earned 708 wins in 1,402 starts, compared with 602 for both workers sent home and 627 for both sent away. Our paired-clear checks the same deployment pattern with a simpler attack. It averaged 0.254 points per round in the six-opponent study, compared with 1.776 for published KISS. The launch is reusable, and the difference tells us to investigate the clear payload and its process growth next.

Both leading Koenigstuhl Tiny sources in our captured table use the same two silk displacements and spend early turns on remote writes. Removing the second-ranked warrior's first ten writes while preserving its operands reduced its score against all four tested opponents. The detailed review includes the queue trace and a comparison with our shorter copy-burst recipe.

In Consonance, one Y hop and three X hops land a copy one cell past an earlier stage. The tiny-resonant-paper recipe lets you move that return by one cell and trace which instruction it overwrites. In our example, the return at +2 ended the original sweep sooner and still scored higher in seven of eight matched comparisons.

The staged Tiny LP recipe keeps the same two-site boot and gives each worker a bounded SPL pass followed by a faster DAT sweep. It raised the six-opponent average from 0.254 to 0.936 points per round. Packing the selector also lets this larger payload share a 45-cell image with a stone.

Scan hits, traps, clear writes and defense controls

Coordinate four LP scanners

Disciples of the Void uses two loaders to start four scanners by turn 45. Opposite strides and A-field tests let the scanners operate for thousands of turns before the first hit on a live partner. Later, a scanner can lock processes in place with JMP and switch into a DAT clear.

Build lp-opposed-scanners to compare one, two or four probe lanes. The link check rejects a lane that intersects friendly code, and the quiet gate checks the complete orbit. On the six-opponent replay, the four-lane JMP version scored 0.452 points per round, compared with 0.158 for DAT at the same addresses.

The published warrior scored 1.621. Forcing its scan guards to jump back into the scan reduced that to 0.727. The replay also traces a case where a paper changes the guard's pointer, triggers the clear early, and turns a tie into a win for the scanner.

The lp-opposed-handoff experiment writes JMP on turn 93 and DAT on turn 101 against a planted target, then clears a 120-cell strip. Switching as soon as the first trap lands scored 0.102 points per round, compared with 0.385 for continuing to scan in the same layout.

The counted handoff gives each scanner its own strike counter while keeping the strip and deployment fixed. Waiting for 32 strikes raised the first panel score to 0.336. With fresh placements, the best counted variant waited for 64 strikes and scored 0.346, compared with 0.354 for continuing to scan.

Outward sweeps let the scanner pairs cover up to 1,800 tested cells without touching friendly code. With the handoff fixed at 64 strikes, widening the sweep gave no clear advantage. The 120-cell and 960-cell outward variants scored 0.191 and 0.186 against four separate published opponents, compared with 0.188 for continued scanning.

The aimed variant moves the 120-cell sweep to the last trap when the full strip fits inside a verified corridor. It tied the fixed-origin control on both panels. Against scatter paper, four of sixteen observed rounds used the aimed origin.

A shorter DAT follow-up still underperformed keeping the JMP locks. In one matched round, leaving the locks intact held up to five enemy processes and earned a tie. The lp-dense-scanners recipe keeps the locks and doubles each probe orbit from 100 to 200 cells. It improved the mean score against both tested fields.

The 26-cell lp-transparent-pair isolates a different idea from the published source. It tests only A fields, passes over its own zero-A JMP traps, and reaches its partner's probe on look 3,997. Its own probe triggers the clear on look 3,999. Changing only the probe modifier to B-only or both fields makes it keep striking its own traps.

The 54-cell lp-transparent-quartet starts two such pairs on opposite parities. At the eight-process cap, each clear has a feeder, a DAT writer, and a continuation queued to die. The two locked forward scanners occupy the remaining slots. A replay that restores only the four writes between the two clearing bodies keeps both writers alive through turn 80,000.

The 68-cell lp-partitioned-quartet keeps both writers active by giving them separate sectors. It keeps the feeder on the continuation that SPL queues first. A same-layout control puts the feeder on the optional child and loses one of its clears when the queue fills.

Protected sectors, FIFO traces and reusable parts

The standalone lp-sector-clear lets multiple clears share the queue while the linker checks their sectors. In a four-clear trace, all four unpaced feeders survived, but the fourth made just four DAT writes. Adding one return turn per feeder brought each site to about 2,960 writes.

Build a five-instruction scanner

Save this original ZoreWars example as nano-lane.red. The look instruction's B field is both the address to inspect and the address attack uses. Adding five makes a 16-cell orbit on an 80-cell ring. Its lane crosses only one of its own instructions, the zero-valued DAT, which the scan skips.

;redcode-nano
;name Nano Lane
;author ZoreWars
;strategy Scan one residue class, sharing the scan's B-field with the attack.
;assert CORESIZE == 80 && MAXLENGTH == 5

        ORG     look
advance ADD.AB  #5, look
look    JMZ.F   advance, @43
attack  MOV.I   bomb, @look
        JMP.B   advance
bomb    DAT.F   $0, $0
        END
node cli/zorewars.js check nano-lane.red -s 80 -c 800 -p 80 -l 5 -d 5 -S 5 --json
node examples/hill-study/replay-nano.mjs
node examples/hill-study/replay-nano.mjs type1.red bombussylvestris.red

The replay script checks the repository example at two load bases, including a wrap, and plants a target with a nonzero A field and zero B field. JMZ.F detects it. JMZ.B skips it. With published opponents supplied, the script runs both variants across all 71 legal relative starts and both first-mover orders. Nano Lane is a small test of a shared-pointer mechanism. In the measured sample it lost most rounds.

To study your own change, keep the opponent source, settings, placement order, and instruction budget fixed. Compare the intended mechanism first, then look at wins, losses, and ties against opponents from more than one era. Use full round traces to learn whether the change found an enemy earlier, wrote the wrong cell, or reached the attack too late.
